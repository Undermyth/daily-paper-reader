Abstract 45 Long-term memory (LTM) formation typically requires extensive training or highly salient 46 experiences, limiting learning efficiency. Operant conditioning is generally thought to produce 47 stronger memory than classical conditioning because of its active learning component. 48 Unexpectedly, however, laser-based social conditioning in Drosophila melanogaster revealed 49 that while classical paradigms yielded lower short-term memory (STM) scores but higher LTM 50 retention, operant paradigms exhibited higher STM scores followed by rapid LTM decay. To 51 resolve this discrepancy, we employed the AI Complex Systems Response (AI-CSR) framework, 52 which reconstructs high-dimensional learning landscapes from sparse sampling and predicts 53 globally optimal training conditions. Following AI-CSR optimization, operant conditioning 54 produced a twofold increase in LTM scores, yielding the strongest 24-hour social memory 55 performance reported in flies to date and revealing the expected superiority of active learning, 56 which had conversely shown poorer performance under standard training protocols. In contrast, 57 AI-CSR did not further enhance classical conditioning performance but reduced training time by 58 50%. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed expanded neuronal recruitment marked by the 59 activation and inhibition of various gene combinations. Together, these findings link circuit-level 60 reorganization with molecular programs underlying efficient long-term memory and demonstrate 61 how AI-guided optimization can uncover latent learning capacity in biological systems. 62

Main 64 Long-term memory (LTM) formation in humans and other animals typically requires repeated 65 training or highly salient experiences 1-7 . In Drosophila melanogaster , a widely used model for 66 memory studies, LTM is usually induced through multiple training cycles 3,8-10 . For example, 67 olfactory classical conditioning requires 5–10 spaced training cycles, each pairing odor exposure 68 with electric shock, to generate memories lasting beyond 24 hours 8; inter-trial intervals are 69 essential for effective LTM formation. In contrast, operant conditioning in Drosophila has been 70 studied primarily in courtship suppression 11,12 , where repeated rejection by mated females over 71 prolonged training periods (typically 6–10 hours) leads to persistent suppression of courtship 72 behavior. 73 Building on our previously developed ALTOMS system 13 , which decomposes social memory 74 into classical and operant components, we examined how experimental parameters shape 75 learning outcomes. We uncovered a striking dissociation between classical and operant 76 conditioning. Classical conditioning scores peaked across a broad parameter range, whereas 77 operant conditioning—particularly long-term memory (LTM)—was confined to a narrow 78 parameter window. Within this window, flies initially exhibited lower learning scores but 79 showed markedly enhanced performance after 24 hours. Operant LTM scores were the highest 80 among all paradigms tested, reaching levels comparable to those typically observed in short-term 81 memory (STM). 82 Efficient learning has long been a central goal in education and neuroscience. Learning protocols 83 depend on multiple interacting variables—including duration, intensity, repetition, and spacing— 84 creating a vast search space. Despite their widespread use, protocol design still lacks a systematic 85 optimization framework; conventional trial-and-error approaches are time-consuming and 86 typically converge to local optima. Drawing on complex systems science, we discovered the AI 87 Complex Systems Response (AI-CSR) equation, a general governing equation that resolves 88

Results 93 

Laser-Based Conditioning Reveals Convergent Long-Term Memory States 94 We established classical and operant conditioning paradigms compatible with the ALTOMS 95 system to train male Drosophila melanogaster (Fig. 1A). In classical conditioning, the 96 conditioned stimulus was the presence of a female, and the unconditioned stimulus was repetitive 97 laser punishment delivered at fixed intervals and intensity. This design parallels olfactory 98 classical conditioning, in which electric shocks are repeatedly paired with odors 8.99 Classical conditioning elicited a robust immediate memory response, with the restraining index 100 (RI) reaching ~60, but declined and stabilized at ~40 from 24 hours to 4 days post-training (Fig. 101 1B). In contrast, operant conditioning used the same conditioned stimulus (female fly) but 102 delivered laser punishment contingent on male behavior, specifically inter-fly distance. To avoid 103 punishment, males had to cease approaching and actively retreat. This action–outcome 104 contingency produced rapid learning, consistent with prior work 13 , yielding acquisition-phase RI 105 values near 80 within 1 hour. Unexpectedly, RI declined to ~40 after 24 hours—matching 106 classical conditioning—and remained stable for up to 4 days (Fig. 1B). 107 This pattern has traditionally been attributed to a ceiling in Drosophila long-term memory, with 108 RI values plateauing near 40, discouraging further optimization. We instead hypothesized that 109 this limit reflects suboptimal training conditions rather than neural capacity. For laser-based 110 conditioning, outcomes depend on multiple parameters—including laser power, punishment bout 111 length, and training duration—and their respective levels, which have been selected largely by 112 heuristic trial and error. In contrast, operant conditioning showed 155 a striking temporal dissociation. Flies trained with the original protocol and tested at 24 h 156 exhibited RI values near 40, and flies trained with OP-LTM Opt showed similarly low 157 performance immediately after training. Remarkably, OP-LTM Opt produced maximal 158 performance at 24 h, revealing a delayed enhancement effect (Fig. 3B). This finding challenges 159 the assumption that optimal LTM training also maximizes short-term memory and suggests that 160 the mechanisms underlying LTM formation differ fundamentally from those supporting short-161 term memory. 162

Discussion 206 Despite these advances, several limitations remain. First, although Hr38 is a robust marker of 207 neuronal activation, it does not establish functional necessity for memory storage. Future studies 208 using genetic silencing or activation of Hr38 -expressing neurons will be required to test causal 209 links between circuit engagement and behavior. Second, although we mapped the spatial 210 distribution of activated neurons, the underlying mechanisms likely involve complex 211 spatiotemporal dynamics. Memory consolidation may depend on sequential recruitment of 212 multiple neuronal ensembles rather than sustained activity within a fixed population. Thus, 213

Tissue expansion 443 Brain tissues were first incubated in a solution containing methacrylic acid N-444 hydroxysuccinimide ester (MA-NHS) at 4 °C for 16–18 h. Following this step, samples were 445 rinsed twice with PBS at room temperature and then immersed in monomer solution at 4 °C for 5 446 min. This immersion was repeated twice to allow sufficient diffusion of the monomer prior to gel 447 polymerization. 448 The tissues were subsequently transferred into a pre-gel solution composed of 2 M sodium 449 chloride (NaCl), 2.5% acrylamide (AA), 0.15% N,N′-methylenebisacrylamide (BIS), 8.625% 450 sodium acrylate (SA), 0.01% 4-hydroxy-TEMPO (4-HT), 0.2% tetramethylethylenediamine 451 (TEMED), and 0.2% ammonium persulfate (APS) in PBS. APS served as the polymerization 452 initiator. Gelation was performed in a custom chamber (~200 μm thick, ~9 μL volume) at 37 °C 453 for approximately 2 h. 454 After gelation, samples were incubated in digestion buffer (50 mM Tris, pH 8.0; 1 mM EDTA; 455 0.5% Triton X-100; 0.8 M guanidine HCl) supplemented with proteinase K (8 U/mL) and gently 456 agitated at room temperature for 16–18 h. Finally, digested samples were equilibrated in distilled 457 water in a 6-cm dish for at least 1 h, with water exchanged every 20 min, until complete isotropic 458 expansion of the hydrogel-embedded tissues was achieved. 459 460
