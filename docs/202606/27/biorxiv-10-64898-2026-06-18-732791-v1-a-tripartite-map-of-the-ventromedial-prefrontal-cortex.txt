# Abstract 36 

The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPFC) has been repeatedly implicated in affect, 37 

valuation, and social cognition, yet how these diverse functions are organized within a 38 

single cortical territory has remained unresolved. Here, we integrate large-scale meta-39 

analysis, individual-level task fMRI, artificial neural-network encoding models, and 40 

multimodal connectivity analyses to reveal the internal functional architecture of the 41 

human VMPFC. Across four complementary studies, we identify a robust tripartite 42 

organization along the anterior–posterior axis, comprising posterior affective, middle 43 

valuation, and anterior social functional motifs. Connectivity fingerprinting demonstrates 44 

that each motif is preferentially embedded within distinct large-scale brain networks, 45 

providing a mechanistic account of VMPFC functional specialization. This organization is 46 

reproducible at the level of individual subjects, generalizes to naturalistic stimuli, extends 47 

across development, and shows cross-species correspondence with non-human primates 48 

and multiple neurobiological markers. Together, these findings resolve a long-standing 49 

organizational question and establish a biologically grounded framework for interpreting 50 

VMPFC function. 51

# Introduction 53 

The human ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPFC) has long been a central focus of 54 

neuroscience owing to its involvement in emotion, decision-making, and social behavior 1 .55 

Early lesion studies, including the historical case of Phineas Gage, revealed its importance 56 

for personality and affective regulation 2 , while subsequent neuroimaging work identified 57 

the VMPFC as one of the most metabolically active and evolutionarily expanded regions 58 

of the human cortex 3,4 . Positioned along the cortical midline and densely interconnected 59 

with distributed systems—including the default mode, limbic, frontoparietal, and salience 60 

networks—the VMPFC occupies a central hub in large-scale brain organization 5 .61 

Functionally, it has been implicated across a wide range of psychological phenomena, 62 

including affective regulation, valuation, self-referential processing, social inference, 63 

memory, and autonomic control 1 , and its dysfunction is associated with multiple 64 

psychiatric conditions such as depression, addiction, psychopathy, and autism 6 . Despite 65 

decades of intensive research, however, a principled account explaining why this single 66 

region consistently recurs across such diverse literatures remains lacking. 67 

Progress toward identifying the functional architecture of the VMPFC has been 68 

hindered by several factors. First, the term “VMPFC” does not correspond to a single 69 

anatomically circumscribed area but spans multiple cytoarchitectonic subdivisions whose 70 

boundaries remain debated 7–9 . Second, the region exhibits pronounced functional 71 

heterogeneity, participating in affective, motivational, and social processes that are often 72 

investigated in isolation 1 . Third, substantial interindividual variability in sulcal 73 

morphology shifts the spatial localization of functional peaks, complicating group-level 74 

inference 10,11 . Finally, the ventral prefrontal cortex is particularly vulnerable to 75 

susceptibility artifacts in conventional fMRI, leading to signal dropout and spatial 76 

distortion unless acquisition is carefully optimized 12–14 . Together, these factors have 77 

contributed to a fragmented literature in which distinct functional claims coexist without 78 

being integrated into a coherent organizational framework. 79 

In other complex association cortices, such fragmentation has been addressed through 80 

data-driven parcellation frameworks that explicitly link anatomy, connectivity, and 81 

function into reproducible subregional maps. By identifying spatially contiguous 82

Although theories of VMPFC function vary widely, prior work has repeatedly 91 

suggested that its functional heterogeneity may reflect multiple interacting processes 92 

related to affect, valuation, and social cognition 1,6,21 . Meta-analytic studies have reported 93 

partially dissociable activation patterns associated with these processes 6,22 , and 94

traditions, and thus remain conceptually suggestive rather than organizationally definitive. 99 

In particular, it has not been established whether such recurring dissociations reflect a 100 

stable and reproducible organizational principle of the VMPFC, or instead arise from 101 

context-dependent task demands, averaging across heterogeneous individuals, or 102 

methodological idiosyncrasies. Critically, prior work has not jointly tested whether 103 

putative VMPFC subdivisions are reproducible at the level of individual brains, generalize 104 

beyond experimenter-defined tasks to naturalistic stimulus spaces, and are grounded in 105 

identifiable large-scale network mechanisms. 106 

Here, we address this gap by testing whether a convergent, tripartite functional 107

VMPFC. Throughout this paper, we use the term functional motif to denote a recurrent and 109

psychological processes while remaining embedded within a continuous and integrative 111 

circuit architecture, rather than a rigid, domain-specific module. Leveraging a set of 112 

complementary, independently grounded approaches—including meta-analytic co-113

# Results 130 

Study 1 | Characterizing the functional diversity of the VMPFC 131 

To delineate the dominant functional associations of the ventromedial prefrontal 132 

cortex (VMPFC), we first performed large-scale reverse-inference and co-activation meta-133 

analyses using the Neurosynth database (n = 14,371 fMRI studies). Reverse-inference 134 

mapping identified the top 15 functional terms most frequently associated with VMPFC 135 

activation (Fig. 1a). These terms exhibited an apparent semantic structure and were 136 

provisionally organized into three broad categories—social, valuation, and affect— 137 

suggesting intrinsic functional heterogeneity (see the full list of top 50 functional terms in 138 

Supplementary Table 1). 139 

To test whether this semantic organization reflects a robust data-driven structure, we 140 

performed additional term-clustering analyses in two independent representational spaces 141 

(Extended Data Fig. 9). First, we embedded the Neurosynth terms using a pretrained 142 

language model and applied hierarchical clustering in semantic space. Second, we clustered 143 

the same terms using their whole-brain Neurosynth association maps, excluding voxels 144 

within the VMPFC mask to avoid circularity with the VMPFC parcellation. Across both 145 

the original top-15 terms and an expanded set of 50 terms, the two approaches recovered a 146 

highly similar three-cluster structure. Finally, an independent language model was used to 147 

assign functional labels to the recovered clusters, yielding labels semantically equivalent 148 

to affect, valuation, and social cognition with high consistency across repeated runs 149 

(Extended Data Fig. 9b). These results provide quantitative support for the tripartite 150 

functional interpretation of the VMPFC motifs while reducing reliance on investigator-151 

defined categorization. 152 

We next applied meta-analytic co-activation modeling (MACM) 27 to test whether this 153 

functional heterogeneity corresponded to separable subregions within the VMPFC. By 154 

clustering whole-brain co-activation patterns for the VMPFC across studies, we generated 155 

parcellation solutions from K = 2 to K = 6 (Fig. 1b). Quantitative model-selection criteria 156 

converged on a three-parcel solution (K = 3), which simultaneously maximized between-157 

cluster separability (Silhouette coefficient) and minimized within-cluster redundancy 158

findings indicate that the observed tripartite structure is a stable property of the underlying 187 

co-activation architecture rather than an artifact of initialization, voxel-selection criteria, 188 

or clustering algorithm. Thus, the tripartite solution reflects a consensus structure recovered 189 

across multiple analytical frameworks rather than a specific outcome of K-means 190 

optimization. 191

To functionally characterize each parcel, we performed reverse-inference decoding of 209 

the meta-analytic activation map associated with each cluster. Word-cloud visualizations 210 

and quantitative radar plots (Fig. 1c–d) revealed a consistent posterior–anterior ordering of 211 

dominant functional associations across the three parcels. Specifically, the posterior 212 

VMPFC (pVMPFC) was preferentially associated with affect-related terms such as fear, 213 

arousal, and threat; the middle VMPFC (mVMPFC) with valuation- and decision-related 214 

terms such as value, money, and choice; and the anterior VMPFC (aVMPFC) with social 215 

and self-referential terms such as theory of mind, mentalizing, and autobiographical 216 

processing. This ordered pattern indicates systematic differentiation in functional 217 

weighting across spatially discrete VMPFC subregions. 218 

Importantly, comparison across alternative parcellation resolutions (K = 2–6; 219 

Extended Data Fig. 2) showed that this tripartite functional structure is preserved across 220 

resolutions: posterior parcels consistently exhibited affect-related dominance, middle 221 

parcels valuation-related dominance, and anterior parcels social-related dominance. 222 

Lower-dimensional solutions (K = 2) merged adjacent motifs into broader, mixed profiles, 223 

whereas higher-dimensional solutions (K ≥ 4) subdivided these motifs into smaller parcels 224 

that retained their dominant functional preferences. Notably, valuation- and social-related 225 

motifs showed greater subdivision at higher K values, whereas the affective motif remained 226 

comparatively cohesive, consistent with its more spatially compact posterior distribution. 227 

In contrast, K = 3 provides the minimal resolution at which posterior affective, middle 228 

valuation, and anterior social motifs are simultaneously isolated as spatially coherent and 229 

functionally distinct subregions, consistent with quantitative model-selection criteria. 230 

Together, these meta-analytic results provide convergent evidence for a tripartite 231 

motif-based functional organization of the VMPFC. This organization integrates affective 232 

processing, value computation, and social cognition along a continuous posterior–anterior 233 

axis, establishing a principled foundation for subsequent individual-level validation and 234 

connectivity-based analyses (Studies 2–4). 235

Study 2 | Validation of the tripartite organization at the individual level 237 

To confirm that the tripartite motif-based organization of the VMPFC extends beyond 238 

meta-analytic aggregation of task-evoked activations across thousands of published fMRI 239 

studies, we next examined individual-level task fMRI (tfMRI) data from the Human 240 

Connectome Project (i.e., HCP Young Adult dataset, n = 667). Three tfMRI paradigms 241 

were selected to probe distinct functional processes associated with the VMPFC: affective 242 

processing (HCP Emotion), value-based decision making (HCP Gambling), and social 243 

cognition (HCP Social) 30 . For visualization, group-level task activation maps revealed a 244 

consistent posterior–anterior ordering of peak responses within the VMPFC (Fig. 2a), 245 

mirroring motif-consistent functional biases: the posterior region co-activated with the 246 

amygdala (AMG), insula (INS), and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) during affective 247 

processing; the middle region with the ventral striatum (VS) and orbitofrontal cortex 248 

(LOFC) during reward processing; and the anterior region with the dorsomedial prefrontal 249 

cortex (DMPFC), temporoparietal junction (TPJ), precuneus (PreC), and anterior temporal 250 

lobe (ATL) during social processing. At the single-subject level, VMPFC peak activations 251 

along the posterior–anterior axis exhibited a robust tri-modal spatial distribution (Fig. 2b). 252 

A k-nearest-neighbor (KNN) classifier—which assigns each subject’s VMPFC activation 253 

peak to one of three task-defined labels based on spatial proximity within a smoothed, 254 

population-derived VMPFC labeling field—reliably discriminated the three functional 255 

motifs, achieving classification accuracies of 0.688 (left VMPFC) and 0.716 (right 256 

VMPFC), far exceeding permutation baselines (~0.33, p < 0.001; see Methods: HCP task 257 

fMRI data and KNN classification ). Importantly, these findings were not specific to the 258 

KNN classifier. Repeating the analyses using Support Vector Classification and Logistic 259 

Regression yielded comparable decoding accuracies in both hemispheres (Supplementary 260 

Fig. 3b), demonstrating that individual-level validation of the tripartite motif structure is 261 

robust across classifier families. These results demonstrate that the tripartite VMPFC 262 

architecture is reproducible across individuals and hemispheres. 263

Although task-based fMRI provides a powerful test of domain specificity, such 291 

paradigms necessarily sample a limited and experimenter-defined set of psychological 292 

conditions. To assess whether the tripartite motif-based organization of the VMPFC 293 

reflects a more general principle of neural representation—one that extends to continuous, 294 

high-dimensional stimulus spaces—we complemented task fMRI with encoding models 295 

trained on naturalistic stimuli. This approach allows us to ask whether VMPFC subregions 296 

exhibit systematic and predictable response preferences when driven by rich visual inputs 297 

that more closely approximate real-world experience. To operationalize this test, we 298 

leveraged artificial neural-network (ANN) encoding models trained on large-scale 299 

naturalistic stimuli (Fig. 2c). Building on recent evidence that deep networks approximate 300 

representational transformations in high-level cortex 31,32 , we trained feature-weighted 301 

receptive-field models (fwRF, see Extended Data Fig. 3a) on the Natural Scenes Dataset 302 

(NSD), which provides 22,000–30,000 stimulus–response pairs from 7T fMRI 33 (see 303 

Methods: ANN-based encoding model ). Separate models predicted the mean response of 304 

the posterior, middle, and anterior VMPFC subregions using hierarchical visual features 305 

extracted from AlexNet, spanning low-level image properties (e.g., edges and textures) to 306 

higher-level object- and scene-related representations. When applied to more than 30,000 307

Because real-world stimuli rarely map cleanly onto a single domain, we next asked 315

with an architecture optimized for integration rather than strict functional segregation. 328 

Together, these results establish that the tripartite VMPFC architecture identified in 329 

Study 1 meta-analysis is replicable at the level of individual HCP subjects, predictable by 330 

ANN models, and systematically modulated by domain combinations, highlighting its 331 

integrative role in linking affective, value-based, and social computations. 332

Study 3 | Connectivity-based mechanisms underlying VMPFC 334 

individual differences 335 

Although Studies 1-2 established a consistent anterior-middle-posterior organization 336 

of the VMPFC at the population level, closer inspection revealed substantial inter-337

individual variability in the precise spatial extent and boundary transitions of these motifs 338 

across participants (Fig. 3a). Understanding the origin of this variability is essential for 339 

identifying the network-level principles that shape VMPFC functional specialization. A 340 

large body of prior work suggests that a region’s functional response properties are strongly 341

Because voxel-wise connectivity-based prediction modelling places more stringent 345 

demands on fMRI signal quality than localization or decoding analyses—particularly in 346 

ventral prefrontal and orbitofrontal cortex, where susceptibility artifacts and signal loss are 347 

prominent 38 —we collected a complementary in-house dataset (n = 42) using an optimized 348 

distortion-corrected acquisition 13 and extended scanning duration 14 to enhance data fidelity 349 

in the VMPFC (see Methods & Supplementary Methods). Importantly, this choice reflects 350 

the increased sensitivity of connectivity–activity mapping to signal-to-noise ratio and data 351 

density, rather than limitations of existing large-scale datasets. The optimized protocol 352 

substantially improved temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) in the VMPFC and yielded 353 

task activation patterns closely matching those observed in the HCP dataset (Extended Data 354 

Fig. 4). For each VMPFC voxel, we then computed structural connectivity (SC) 355 

fingerprints using diffusion tractography and functional connectivity (FC) fingerprints 356 

using resting-state fMRI, defined as voxel-wise connectivity strengths to all parcels in the 357 

brain. 358 

To test whether connectivity architecture predicts functional responses, we used a 359 

voxel-wise least-squares linear regression framework 36 to estimate task-evoked activation 360 

from connectivity fingerprint, applying leave-one-subject-out cross-validation (Fig. 3b). 361

# Discussion 513 

The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPFC) has long been implicated in affect, 514 

decision making, and social cognition, yet a principled account of how these diverse 515 

functions are organized within a single cortical territory has remained elusive. Although 516 

previous studies have reported multiple VMPFC subdivisions (Extended Data Fig. 1), most 517 

have remained largely descriptive, identifying regional boundaries within a particular 518 

dataset or modality without establishing whether those boundaries reflect a general 519 

organizational principle that reproduces across individuals, measurement modalities, 520 

developmental stages, and species. Consequently, it has remained unclear whether the 521 

functional heterogeneity of the VMPFC reflects a collection of partially independent 522 

processes or a coherent underlying architecture. Importantly, the present work addresses a 523 

different level of organization from prior studies that focused on ventral–dorsal 524 

dissociations across the broader medial prefrontal cortex. While ventral and dorsal medial 525 

prefrontal systems are known to differ in their connectivity and functional profile 41 , the 526 

unresolved question addressed here is whether the VMPFC itself contains an internal 527

affective, valuation-related, and social functions. 529 

The present findings suggest that this heterogeneity is organized by a reproducible 530 

anterior–middle–posterior motif architecture that generalizes across individuals, modalities, 531 

developmental stages, and species. Accordingly, the primary contribution of the present 532 

study is not the identification of three parcels per se, but the demonstration that a common 533 

tripartite motif architecture provides a reproducible organizational scaffold linking 534 

functional specialization, connectivity architecture, development, and multimodal 535 

neurobiology within the VMPFC. Several observations support this conclusion. First, using 536 

cross-validated connectivity-to-activity mapping, we show that voxel-wise functional 537 

topography within the VMPFC can be predicted from whole-brain connectivity 538 

fingerprints in individual participants, indicating that the tripartite organization emerges 539 

systematically from large-scale network architecture rather than from group-level 540 

averaging. Second, the same organizational structure converges across multiple 541 

independent modalities—including meta-analytic co-activation, task fMRI, ANN-based 542 

encoding models, structural and functional connectivity, and chemoarchitectonic 543 

features—demonstrating that the motifs are not specific to any single measurement 544 

framework. Third, the ANN-based naturalistic analyses show that these motifs generalize 545 

beyond constrained laboratory paradigms and exhibit systematic combinatorial recruitment 546 

when affective, value-related, and social information co-occur within the same stimuli. 547 

Together, these findings provide a unifying account of the diverse functions historically 548 

attributed to the VMPFC, suggesting that affective, motivational, and social processes 549 

reflect coordinated variation within a common large-scale architecture rather than a 550 

collection of unrelated psychological functions. More broadly, the tripartite motif 551 

framework offers a biologically grounded organizational scaffold for understanding 552 

VMPFC function across cognitive, developmental, and comparative levels of analysis. 553

cross-modal empirical convergence—two complementary criteria for a biologically 555

broad domain labels with such model-derived variables would move the present framework 742

organization 67,68 . Fourth, the reverse-inference analyses are inherently constrained by the 744

# Methods 766 

Definition of the VMPFC mask 767 

To ensure anatomical precision and comparability across studies, we adopted a 768 

VMPFC mask defined by a collection of experts as part of their Nature Neuroscience 769 

viewpoints article on the human VMPFC 1 . This mask was based on the Harvard–Oxford 770 

atlas, encompassing the frontal pole, medial and orbital frontal cortex, anterior cingulate, 771 

paracingulate, and subcallosal cortex, while excluding voxels dorsal to z = 0 mm or lateral 772 

to |x| > 12 mm. This definition has been widely adopted in meta-analytic work. Alternative 773 

VMPFC masks used in the literature 45,59,69,70 produce similar coverage but differ in spatial 774 

extent; the present choice ensures maximal overlap with established meta-analytic 775 

conventions and avoids inclusion of dorso-medial or orbito-lateral territories. 776 

The present study focused specifically on the VMPFC rather than the entire medial 777 

prefrontal cortex (MPFC) because prior work has already established robust functional and 778 

connectivity dissociations between ventral and dorsal medial prefrontal systems. 779 

Accordingly, the primary unresolved question motivating the present work was not 780 

whether ventral and dorsal MPFC differ, but whether the VMPFC itself contains a 781 

reproducible internal organization capable of accounting for its heterogeneous involvement 782 

across affective, valuation-related, and social functions. Our ROI definition was therefore 783 

hypothesis-driven and designed to isolate the ventromedial territory most consistently 784 

implicated across these functions while avoiding extension into broader dorsomedial 785 

systems that operate at a different anatomical and functional scale. 786

Parcellation based on Meta-analytic co-activation modeling (MACM) 824 

Meta-analytic co-activation modeling (MACM) was used to quantify how frequently 825 

VMPFC voxels co-activate with other brain regions across diverse tasks 27,70 . All analyses 826 

were performed in standard 2-mm MNI152 voxel space without using a predefined 827 

anatomical atlas, following established Neurosynth-based parcellation procedures 73,74 .828 

The analysis was based on the full Neurosynth database (version 0.7; N = 14,371 829 

studies). For each voxel in the brain, Neurosynth provides a study-wise activation profile 830 

indicating the studies in which that voxel was reported as active. To reduce noise and 831 

improve reliability, voxels associated with fewer than 150 studies were excluded. This 832 

procedure yielded 1,784 voxels within the VMPFC mask and 129,213 voxels outside the 833 

VMPFC. 834 

For each VMPFC voxel, a whole-brain co-activation profile was computed by 835 

quantifying its co-activation relationship with every non-VMPFC voxel across studies. 836 

This produced a co-activation matrix of size 1,784 × 129,213, in which rows corresponded 837 

to VMPFC voxels and columns corresponded to non-VMPFC voxels. To reduce 838 

dimensionality and computational complexity, principal component analysis (PCA) was 839 

applied to this matrix and the first 100 principal components were retained. Each VMPFC 840 

voxel was therefore represented by a 100-dimensional co-activation feature vector 841 

summarizing its relationship with large-scale whole-brain activation patterns. The resulting 842 

dissimilarity matrix (1 – Pearson’s r) quantified voxel-wise differences between co-843 

activation profiles. 844 

Voxels were grouped using K-means clustering into solutions ranging from K = 2 to 845 

K = 6 parcels. To minimize sensitivity to initialization, clustering was performed with 846 

1,000 random initializations and the solution with the lowest within-cluster variance was 847 

retained 73,75 . Optimal cluster number was evaluated using multiple validity metrics, 848 

including the Silhouette coefficient and Davies–Bouldin index 42,76 . The K = 3 solution 849 

simultaneously maximized cluster cohesion and separation, yielded spatially contiguous 850 

subregions arranged along the posterior–anterior axis (Fig. 1b), and showed the strongest 851

Functional decoding of VMPFC parcels 854 

For MACM-derived VMPFC parcels, each parcel’s psychological profile was 855 

characterized by repeating the reverse-inference analysis within its submask. For each 856 

parcel, we extracted the highest-ranking functional terms and correlated its spatial map 857 

with Neurosynth term-based maps using Pearson correlation. The strength of each domain 858 

association was visualized as radar plots (Fig. 1d), allowing direct comparison across social, 859 

valuation, and affect axes. Across alternative K solutions (K = 2–6), we further generated 860 

word-cloud visualizations summarizing the top decoding terms per parcel. These analyses 861 

revealed that posterior parcels were consistently dominated by affect-related terms, middle 862 

parcels by valuation-related terms, and anterior parcels by social-related terms across 863 

resolutions. Lower-dimensional solutions (K = 2) merged these motifs into broader, mixed 864 

profiles, whereas higher-dimensional solutions (K ≥ 4) subdivided them into smaller 865 

parcels that retained domain preferences but showed increased fragmentation. In contrast, 866

For resting-state FC (rsFC) ‑derived VMPFC parcels, we performed the same 870 

term‑based decoding on parcel‑specific resting‑state connectivity maps. For each parcel 871

parcel weights by dividing each weight by the sum across parcels, yielding proportional 878

algorithms. K-means was selected as the primary clustering approach because it is widely 1063 

used in connectivity-based and meta-analytic brain parcellation studies, facilitates 1064 

comparison with prior MACM investigations, and provides a transparent baseline method 1065 

for identifying large-scale motif structure in low-dimensional co-activation spaces. 1066 

First, we varied the minimum-study inclusion threshold used for voxel selection in 1067 

the MACM analysis (≥100, ≥150, and ≥200 studies per voxel), yielding VMPFC masks 1068 

containing 2,211, 1,784, and 1,403 voxels, respectively. For each threshold, K-means 1069 

clustering (K = 3) was repeated 100 times using independent random seeds. Spatial 1070 

similarity between solutions was quantified using Dice coefficients relative to the 1071 

consensus tripartite solution. 1072 

To assess dependence on algorithm choice, we repeated the parcellation analyses 1073 

using Spectral Clustering and Agglomerative Clustering. For each algorithm, clustering 1074 

was repeated 100 times and spatial similarity to the original K-means solution was 1075 

quantified using Dice coefficients. Representative maps corresponding to the lowest-1076 

similarity solutions were retained for visualization to provide a conservative estimate of 1077 

robustness. 1078

the Neurosynth dataset and whether the observed tripartite organization could be 1080 

influenced by biases in the composition of the literature, we repeated the entire parcellation 1081

which is automatically constructed from high-frequency terms extracted from published 1083 

articles, BrainMap is manually curated and provides human expert annotations of 1084 

experimental paradigms and behavioral domains. Coordinate data were retrieved using 1085 

Sleuth and converted into the same 2-mm MNI152 voxel space used for the Neurosynth 1086 

analyses. The BrainMap database contained 4,341 studies and 22,301 experiments. 1087

In-house Data with Optimized MRI Acquisition Details 1600 

All MRI data were collected at the Beijing Normal University Brain Imaging Center 1601 

using a Siemens MAGNETOM Prisma 3T scanner equipped with a 64-channel head coil. 1602 

Functional images were acquired using a T2-weighted gradient-echo echo-planar imaging 1603 

(EPI)* sequence with multiband acceleration. To reduce susceptibility-induced signal 1604 

dropout in ventral prefrontal regions, slices were acquired with a 30° tilt relative to the 1605 

AC–PC line, providing improved signal coverage in the VMPFC. Each functional volume 1606 

consisted of 72 axial slices (whole-brain coverage), with an in-plane resolution of 2 × 2 1607 

mm² and slice thickness of 2 mm (no gap). Acquisition parameters were: TR = 2000 ms, 1608 

TE = 22 ms, flip angle = 90°, phase-encoding direction = anterior–posterior, FOV = 200 × 1609 

200 mm², bandwidth = 2380 Hz/Px, and multiband acceleration factor = 2. 1610 

A high-resolution T1-weighted structural image was acquired using a 3D MPRAGE 1611 

sequence (voxel size = 1 × 1 × 1 mm³; TR = 2530 ms, TE = 2.27 ms, TI = 1100 ms, flip 1612 

angle = 7°, FOV = 256 × 256 mm²). To correct for geometric distortions in the EPI data, a 1613 

field map was acquired using a dual-echo spin-echo sequence (matched slice prescription; 1614 

voxel size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³; TR = 720 ms; TE₁ = 4.92 ms, TE₂ = 7.38 ms, flip angle = 60°). 1615 

For diffusion MRI distortion correction, an additional reverse phase-encoded (PA) field 1616 

map was acquired using the same parameters without diffusion weighting. 1617 

To evaluate the improvement afforded by this optimized acquisition protocol, 1618 

temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) was computed using the Nipype confounds module 1619 

(https://nipype.readthedocs.io). Compared to publicly available HCP data, the optimized 1620 

protocol yielded substantially higher tSNR in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (Extended 1621 

Data Fig. 4), confirming the effectiveness of the acquisition adjustments for studying 1622 

VMPFC function. 1623

Multimodal Feature–Based Parcellation 1643 

To examine whether the tripartite organization of the ventromedial prefrontal cortex 1644 

(VMPFC) could be recovered from imaging modalities beyond task activity and brain 1645 

connectivity, we employed four independent profile-based analyses: gray-matter volume 1646 

(GMV), cortical myelination, resting-state brain entropy (BEN), and neurotransmitter 1647 

receptor/transporter distribution. Each modality was processed independently within its 1648 

respective cohort, and voxel- or vertex-wise feature covariance profiles were used for 1649 

clustering analyses (K = 2–6, see Extended Data Fig. 8). 1650 

Gray-Matter Volume (GMV) A profile-based approach using inter-individual 1651 

variations in GMV was applied separately to HCP-YA dataset. Modulated GMV maps 1652 

were derived from participants’ T1-weighted images following the standard voxel-based 1653 

morphometry (VBM) framework (Ashburner & Friston, 2000). This included tissue 1654 

segmentation, spatial normalization to the MNI152NLin2009cAsym template, and 1655 

> .CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0 International license available under a (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted June 19, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.18.732791 doi: bioRxiv preprint 63

modulation using Jacobian determinants to preserve local volumetric information. We 1656 

additionally followed the FSLVBM pipeline, creating a study-specific GM template 1657 

(fslvbm_2_template) and non-linearly registering individual GM maps using 1658 

fslvbm_3_proc. Spatial smoothing employed a Gaussian kernel (FWHM = 6 mm for HCP-1659 

YA). We computed each VMPFC voxel’s covariance with every brain voxel across 1660 

participants, then calculated a voxel-by-voxel cross-correlation matrix describing the 1661 

similarity of whole-brain GMV covariance profiles within the VMPFC. The resulting 1662 

matrices were clustered using k-means (K = 2–6); cluster validity was assessed via 1663 

Silhouette and Davies–Bouldin indices. Guided by prior hypotheses and meta-analytic 1664 

evidence, K = 3 was selected as the optimal solution. 1665 

Cortical Myelination A second approach used cortical myelination (T1w/T2w ratio) 1666 

to define VMPFC subregions in the HCP-YA dataset. Myelin maps were obtained from 1667 

the standard release file MyelinMap_BC.32k_fs_LR.dscalar.nii distributed with the HCP-1668 

YA dataset. Using Connectome Workbench (v2.0.1), the volumetric VMPFC mask 1669 

(defined in MNI space) was projected to the 32k_fs_LR surface and separated into left and 1670 

right hemispheres. For each participant, vertex-wise myelination index values within the 1671 

VMPFC were extracted to form subject-specific feature vectors. Covariance profiles were 1672 

then computed across participants for each vertex relative to the entire cortical surface, 1673 

yielding a group-level vertex-by-vertex similarity matrix. This matrix was clustered using 1674 

k-means (K = 2–6), yielding surface-based myelin-derived subregions. 1675 

Brain Entropy (BEN) Another profile-based analysis leveraged BEN to delineate 1676 

VMPFC subregions. BEN quantifies the irregularity and complexity of spontaneous brain 1677 

activity (Wang et al., 2014). Analyses were conducted on HCP-YA dataset. For each 1678 

participant, BEN maps were generated from preprocessed resting-state fMRI data using 1679 

the BENtbx toolbox with a window length = 3 and a cutoff threshold = 0.6 (Wang et al., 1680 

2014). HCP-YA fMRI data followed the consortium’s minimal preprocessing, then 1681 

underwent additional band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz) and Gaussian smoothing (FWHM 1682 

= 6 mm). BEN maps were smoothed with a 6 mm Gaussian kernel. We computed voxel-1683 

wise covariance between each VMPFC voxel’s BEN profile and all brain voxels, 1684
