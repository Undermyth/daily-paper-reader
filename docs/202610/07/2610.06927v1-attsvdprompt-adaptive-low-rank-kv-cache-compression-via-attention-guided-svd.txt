ABSTRACT 

The key-value (KV) cache of autoregressive transformers grows linearly with con-text length and dominates memory at long context. Most training-free remedies evict low-importance tokens, an irreversible choice along the sequence axis. We instead keep every token and store it more cheaply along the “ feature ” axis. We therefore propose AttSVD , a new interpretable low-rank compression whose ba-sis is derived from each prompt’s own attention geometry: an online, per-prompt truncated SVD that keeps only the directions attention actually reads, cutting per-sistent per-head KV memory in proportion to the retained rank. We propose two decode-time caching strategies, accumulating and streaming, for short and long generation regimes. Furthermore, we propose two refinements that make com-pression adaptive. A per-matrix energy rule sizes the logit space and the attention mass independently. An attention-aware basis truncates only in the spaces atten-tion actually reads, preserving both the attention logits and the attention output. The same factors also provide free, per-head interpretability insights into the ef-fective rank and the geometry attention consumes. Across multiple models, on both an agentic benchmark and the full LongBench suite AttSVD stays on par with the dense cache while using up to ∼50% of the KV-cache memory. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Training-free KV-cache compression methods fall into two families. Eviction methods (e.g. H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), SnapKV (Li et al., 2024), KeyDiff (Park et al., 2025), KVzip (Kim et al., 2025)) keep a budget of tokens and drop the rest entirely; they differ only in how they score impor-tance. Eviction is a hard, irreversible choice along the sequence axis: a wrongly dropped token can never be attended to again. Reconstruction methods (e.g. VECTOR (Lin et al., 2026), Palu (Chang et al., 2025)) instead keep all positions but store them approximately. We propose AttSVD , which is not only a feature-axis compression method but also introduces two decode-time caching strategies for different generation regimes. The core rationale builds on the observed fact that a prompt’s keys and values are effectively low-rank, the keys especially (Saxena et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025); we extend this low-rank observation from the K and V spaces to the attention logit space and the attention mass space. AttSVD computes an online attention-aware factorization in the subspace attention actually reads (rather than by plain spectral energy), per layer and per key/value head, both at prefill (factoring the prompt bulk) and during decode (the streaming scenario), where generated tokens are factored once they age out of a short recent-token window so long generations stay compressed as well (Figure 1). Because the basis is the SVD of the prompt’s own activations, it is, by Eckart-Young (Eckart & Young, 1936), the best possible rank-r approxima-tion of that prompt’s attention logits and attention mass, obtained with no calibration corpus and no training; no token is discarded, and every position is still attended to, just from reconstructed rank-r

factors. Our contributions are: • A per-prompt, calibration-free low-rank KV cache that retains all token positions and is exact at full rank, with a dynamic per-matrix rank rule that sizes keys and values separately so memory adapts to each prompt instead of a fixed schedule; • An attention-aware basis that truncates in the subspace attention actually reads, preserving both the attention logits and the attention output; 1arXiv:2610.06927v1 [cs.LG] 3 Oct 2026 Manuscript under review • Two decode-time caching strategies that both compress at prefill but differ at decode: an ac-cumulating mode for long-prompt, short-decode scenarios (e.g., no-think mode) that keeps generated tokens exact, and a streaming mode for long-prompt, long-decode scenarios (e.g., think mode) that compresses aged-out tokens into thin blocks for a bounded cache. • Interpretability at no extra cost: because the basis is derived from each prompt’s own at-tention geometry, the retained per-matrix ranks and attention-mass profile also serve as per-head diagnostics, exposing each head’s effective-rank compressibility, query-subspace focus, and token saliency without calibration. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Most low-rank KV-cache methods extract the basis ahead of time and reuse it for every prompt. 

Weight decompositions factor the key/value projection weights offline (ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023), Palu (Chang et al., 2025), LoRC (Zhang et al., 2024)); calibration methods estimate the basis from a corpus (Eigen Attention (Saxena et al., 2024), ReCalKV (Yan et al., 2025), KQ-SVD (Lesens et al., 2026)); and cross-layer or trained methods share or learn it (xKV (Chang et al., 2026), MatryoshkaKV (Lin et al., 2025)). OjaKV (Zhu et al., 2026) is also online and per-context but 

approximate and variance-greedy . It keeps a running incremental-PCA basis seeded from a small calibration set, using attention only to pick which tokens stay full-rank rather than to shape its basis. 

How AttSVD differs. Prior methods either extract or learn a single basis reused for every prompt (the offline families above), or track an approximate running basis seeded from calibration (OjaKV). 

AttSVD differs on three axes. (i) Per-prompt and exact. It computes the Eckart-Young-optimal truncated SVD of this prompt’s keys and values at prefill, with no calibration corpus, no training, and no running-basis bookkeeping; the basis is then frozen during generation (unlike OjaKV’s seeded, incremental estimate). (ii) Attention-guided, not variance-greedy. Rather than keeping the directions of largest K, V variance, it truncates in the space attention actually consumes; weighting keys by the query covariance CQ=Q⊤Q (which preserves the logits QK ⊤) and values by per-token attention mass (which preserves the output P V ), so the retained subspace is functional rather than merely descriptive. This is a distinct form of attention-awareness from KQ-SVD: rather than low-rank-approximating the products KQ ⊤ and V W O learned offline from calibration, AttSVD keeps the ordinary per-matrix factors and merely reweights their SVD metric, so one calibration-free rule aligns both keys (via CQ, preserving the scores) and values (via attention mass, preserving the output) with this prompt’s own attention. (iii) Dynamic per-matrix rank. A spectral-energy rule factors each matrix independently from the data so that memory adapts to each prompt instead of following a fixed schedule. For a comprehensive related work discussion, see Appendix A. 

3 METHOD           

> Motivation: Existing KV-cache methods save memory by evicting tokens, permanently discarding their keys and values. We instead retain every token in a cheaper form, re-expanded transiently at inference time, with no eviction or training. Unlike other low-rank reconstruction methods, we aim at a low-rank compression that is per-matrix adaptive ,calibration-free ,prompt-adaptive , robust across prefill & decode length scenarios , and attention-importance aware .

This composes with any rank rule of Section 3.4: the weighting sets the basis, the rule sets the rank. 3.3 TWO KV-CACHE COMPRESSION REGIMES TAILORED TO PREFILL AND DECODE LENGTH 

AttSVD always compresses the prompt at prefill ; the two regimes differ only in how the cache is maintained during decode . We first describe the shared prefill phase, then the two decode variants: an accumulating and a streaming cache. 

Prefill. Given the prompt’s eK, eV for a layer, split off a small recent window of the newest w

tokens, kept exact; call the remaining bulk K(b), V (b) ∈ R(S−w)×d. AttentionSVD each via Eq. (2) into (A eK , B eK ) and (AeV , B eV ) and store only the factors in a compressed side cache, writing just the exact tail to the exact cache. The prefill forward itself is served with the exact K, V that are already in hand; reconstruction at prefill would only inject a lossy step we do not need, so prefill output is bit-identical to the dense cache. The compressed factors are a lossy approximation, consulted only when those tokens are later reconstructed at decode. 

Exact recent-window tail. The newest w tokens are stored exactly rather than decomposed. They carry the highest-impact local context, and a handful of tokens cannot be usefully compressed by a rank-r SVD (and a single decode token is appended one at a time, where factoring is counter-productive). Setting w = 0 recovers a fully decomposed prefill. 4Manuscript under review 

Decode. At each generation step the decomposed bulk is re-materialized transiently as bK(b) =

A eK B eK ∈ R(S−w)×d and bV (b) = AeV BeV (the stored B factors carry the reverse map, so these prod-ucts approximate the original K(b), V (b), not eK, eV ), concatenated with the exact tail and consumed by attention, which forms P from QK ⊤ and O = P V on the recovered K, V ; these tensors exist for one layer at a time and are freed on return, so the transient peak is O(Sd ), never the whole cache. Only the low-rank factors persist across steps. The two strategies differ in decode-time caching: 

Decode strategy 1: accumulating cache. The prefill factors are fixed and each new token is appended exactly to the cache, never compressed; no decode-time SVD is performed and the decode-factor side cache stays empty, so the cache grows with generation length. This is best when the prompt is long but generation is short (e.g. multiple-choice tasks or no-think mode), where the exact generated tokens stay a small fraction of the cache. 

Decode strategy 2: streaming cache (sliding window). Streaming AttSVD bounds the decode-time footprint by compressing during decode (Figure 1, bottom), holding the cache at a fixed size regardless of output length, which suits long think -mode generations. It maintains a sliding exact window of the most recent w tokens: each generated token is appended to the window, and once the window overflows by a full chunk of ℓ tokens (the streaming branch of Algorithm 1, App. C), the oldest ℓ tokens (still held exactly ) are factored with the same decomposition into a per-layer list of frozen decode blocks , then dropped from the window. Decode attention runs on the full sequence, formed by concatenation ( ⊕) of prefill bulk ⊕ decode blocks ⊕ exact window in position order. Two properties keep it sound: aged-out tokens are factored from their exact values (never by compressing the already-lossy prefill bulk), so error does not compound across the generation, and each block is frozen, so a token is compressed at most once. The scheme composes with any rank rule of Section 3.4 and the attention-aware basis of Section 3.2. 3.4 CHOOSING THE RANK : COMPRESSION -RATIO OR PER -MATRIX ENERGY 

AttSVD offers two strategies for setting the rank, each suited to a different use case: a (1) fixed compression ratio c when a specific memory budget is required, and a (2) per-matrix energy rule 

when factor denoising and minimizing reconstruction error matter more than hitting a target budget. 

Compression-ratio rank (unified). If an absolute rank r or a compression ratio c is supplied, both 

K and V use the same r (with r = round((1 − c)d) for a ratio), recovering the basic decompo-sition of Eq. (2). This uniform setting trades away per-matrix adaptivity for an exact, predictable budget; note that c selects a rank (a fraction of the d feature directions), not a fraction of tokens ,so all S tokens are retained. This is the operating point used for the matched-memory settings in Section D.3 (e.g. c=0 .5 ⇒ r=64 ). The two ranks may also be set individually as explicit r eK and 

reV , overriding the shared value; this exposes the per-matrix budget as a direct hyperparameter and enables controlled study of the asymmetric r eK ≪ reV regime. 

Per-matrix energy rank (dynamic). Keys and values are not equally compressible. Building on the pre-RoPE-key observation of ShadowKV (Sun et al., 2025) that keys and values differ in their intrinsic compression capacity, the attention-weighted matrices we factor, eK and eV , likewise likely differ, so a single shared rank over-spends on eK and under-serves eV (or vice versa) and the two may need different ranks. Here AttSVD lets each matrix choose its own rank from its own spectrum. > Insight. By compressing in the score and output space, weighting Kby the query covariance CQ=Q⊤Q
> and Vby the per-token attention mass, AttSVD retains a head’s functional subspace. Because this basis is derived from the prompt’s own attention geometry rather than from fixed weights or calibration data, these same quantities also serve as free, per-head interpretability insights: they reveal how each head geometrically uses its context, rather than what it attends to semantically.

(i) Effective rank. Roy & Vetterli (2007) observe that the effective rank of a matrix such as K or 

V measures its intrinsic dimensionality . Because our compression happens in the score and output geometry, the per-matrix ranks r eK , r eV of Eq. (5) report the intrinsic dimensionality of the scores 

P (through the query-weighted keys eK) and of the output O (through the mass-weighted values 

eV ). The per-matrix ranks can be read as the intrinsic compression capacity of each matrix, and the per-(layer, head) rank map shows which heads carry low-rank, highly compressible context. We empirically observe that, on the models we test, the query- and mass-weightings redistribute singular-value energy yet still leave a systematic gap r eK ≪ reV (Appendix D, Figures 5 and 6). 

(ii) Query subspace. The covariance CQ = Q⊤Q is the subspace the head’s queries actually probe; the decay of its spectrum quantifies how focused (few-directional) vs. diffuse a head’s attention is. 

(iii) Token saliency. The per-key mass ai = P 

> q

Pqi is a token-importance profile (the same heavy-hitter signal exploited by eviction (Zhang et al., 2023)) emerged here freely, so even an eviction-only pipeline can read off which tokens to keep and which to drop. We emphasize that this is geometric , not semantic . The basis vectors are singular directions, not named concepts, they mix position and content (post-RoPE), and they describe each prompt and head rather than explain a prediction. 4 EXPERIMENTS 

Baselines. We compare AttSVD against three reference points at a token budget matched to 

AttSVD ’s memory at each compression setting, so every method shares the same model, prompts, decoding, and cache size. The dense cache is the uncompressed upper bound. We compare against 

eviction methods: H2O (heavy-hitter attention mass), KeyDiff (attention-free cosine similarity), and KVzip (query-agnostic reconstruction scoring); and low-rank compression methods: SVD on raw KV (standard top-r truncated SVD applied directly to K and V , without AttSVD ’s attention weighting), KQ-SVD (attention-aware but from an offline calibration set), and OjaKV (an online 

Oja’s-rule basis seeded from calibration). 

Datasets and benchmarks. We evaluate on two complementary benchmarks .

(i) Agentic benchmark : 184 prompts spanning 13 common scenarios across three difficulty tiers, each annotated with the correct action to take (from ∼77 candidates) or no action when none applies, examining action selection and necessity . An LLM judge scores each output 1 to 5; we report the mean judge score and pass@4 (fraction ≥ 4). Prompts are short but the reasoning-and-response generations are long (median ∼800 tokens, up to ∼10 K). 

(ii) LongBench (Bai et al., 2024): the full public long-context suite ( 1,750 examples over 9 tasks), each scored by its native metric; we report per-task accuracy and the average ( 0 to 100 ). Its long-context inputs and outputs stress the cache at real context length and exercise the streaming sched-ule’s sliding window. 5 CONCLUSION 

AttSVD shows that the KV cache can be compressed without discarding tokens: it retains every token yet stores each in an instance-optimal rank-r subspace obtained from an online, per-prompt 

SVD and reconstructed lazily at decode, so the persistent cache shrinks to ≈r/d of dense. Its contributions are threefold: an online, calibration-free basis that is provably optimal (Eckart-Young) for the prompt at hand; adaptive per-matrix ranks and an attention-aware objective that spend the budget where attention actually concentrates; and accumulating and streaming decode modes for different generation scenarios. Because the factors are exact, they are also directly interpretable : the effective-rank map exposes the intrinsic compressibility of the query-weighted keys eK and mass-weighted values eV , the query subspace shows each head’s focus, and the attention-mass profile flags salient tokens, all without calibration. Empirically, across several models, AttSVD matches the dense cache on the agentic benchmark and LongBench at roughly half the KV-cache memory, positioning instance-optimal reconstruction as a strong, training-free alternative to eviction. 9Manuscript under review

A EXTENDED RELATED WORK 

Eviction methods. Eviction methods take an orthogonal route, keeping a token budget and drop-ping the rest by scoring tokens on accumulated heavy-hitter attention mass (H2O (Zhang et al., 2023)), attention-free key cosine similarity (KeyDiff (Park et al., 2025)), or query-agnostic recon-struction importance (KVzip (Kim et al., 2025)); we compare against all three. A parallel line keeps eviction as the backbone but relaxes its binary keep/drop decision into a non-binary re-tain / approximate / evict allocation (D2O (Wan et al., 2025), ARKV (Lei & Ilager, 2026)). 

Compression and reconstruction methods. Most methods extract the basis ahead of time and reuse it for every prompt; Table 5 groups them by basis source. Weight decompositions apply an offline SVD to the key/value projection weight matrices (ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023), Palu (Chang et al., 2025), LoRC (Zhang et al., 2024)). Calibration methods estimate the basis from a corpus (Eigen Attention (Saxena et al., 2024), ReCalKV (Yan et al., 2025)); KQ-SVD (Lesens et al., 2026) is closest in spirit, factoring the query-key product KQ ⊤ to target the attention scores rather than K

alone, but still from an offline calibration set of queries fixed across prompts. Cross-layer or trained 

methods share or learn the basis (xKV (Chang et al., 2026), MatryoshkaKV (Lin et al., 2025), Stiefel-manifold projections (Benfenati et al., 2026), EliteKV (Zhou et al., 2025)). All of these tie the basis to weights, a calibration distribution, or a learning phase. ShadowKV (Sun et al., 2025) is per-prompt, applying an online SVD to the pre-RoPE keys with no calibration, but it compresses keys only and offloads the full values to CPU. ThinK (Xu et al., 2025) is likewise per-prompt, pruning key channels by query-driven scoring with no training (keys only). OjaKV (Zhu et al., 2026) is also online and per-context, i.e., it maintains a running incremental-PCA basis (Oja’s rule), seeded from a small calibration set and re-orthonormalized during decoding, and uses attention only to choose which tokens stay full-rank rather than to shape the basis, trading exactness for within-generation adaptation. A separate cache-style line predicts or residual-codes values from keys via a fixed map V ≈ KW fit offline (DeltaKV (Hao et al., 2026)), keeping keys and approximating values; these share our “keep all positions” philosophy but with a fixed, generic map. In the same reconstruction-and-compression vein, VECTOR (Lin et al., 2026) reconstructs evicted values from retained keys by least squares, and Attention Matching (Zweiger et al., 2026) fits the compressed cache to match the original attention output rather than the raw K, V ; orthogonally, adaptive KV 

quantization lowers the bit-width of the stored cache rather than its rank (AQUA-KV (Shutova et al., 2025)). These are complementary to AttSVD , which instead sets the per-prompt low-rank basis itself from this prompt’s own attention. D EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND EXTENDED EXPERIMENTS 

On a small model we verify that full-rank ( r=d) decomposition reproduces the dense-attention out-put bit-for-bit ; the reconstruction error then decreases monotonically as r increases, consistent with Eq. (1). Table 1 reports the KV-cache footprint on Phi-4-14B: the measured ratio matches the theo-retical r/d to the decimal at 32 K context; at shorter context the fixed rd and exact-tail terms make the ratio slightly larger, exactly as Eq. (6) predicts. D.1 STREAMING VS . ACCUMULATING DECODE 

The accumulating and streaming schedules differ only in the decode tail. Accumulating keeps ev-ery earlier decode token exact (an O(g) tail that grows with the generation), while streaming factors aged-out tokens into frozen rank-r blocks so the exact buffer stays bounded. The agentic benchmark generations are mostly too short to overflow the w-token window, so streaming rarely evicts; to stress it where it genuinely compresses decode history we repeat the comparison on the full LongBench suite ( n=1 ,750 ), whose long-context outputs routinely overflow the window and trigger repeated chunk compression (Table 6). Across all ranks, streaming stays within 0.3 points of the accumu-lating (decode-exact) cache on average ( ∆ ∈ [−0.29 , +0 .24] ), a gap well inside run-to-run noise, and it follows the dense cache ( 36 .7) down to the 50% budget. This holds even though stream-ing repeatedly factors aged-out tokens into frozen low-rank blocks rather than keeping them exact. Across both a short-output agentic eval (Figure 3) and a long-output academic suite, then, bounding the O(g) decode tail to a fixed window costs no measurable quality. Per task, the two schedules are in fact identical on 8 of 9 LongBench tasks and differ only on GovReport, the sole task with a long ( 512 -token) generation budget, where streaming compression fires once the generation over-flows the w=64 window; the remaining tasks generate ≤ 128 tokens (mostly ≤ 64 ) and end early under greedy decoding, so the streaming path never fires and reduces exactly to accumulating. At the aggressive r=64 the streaming cache holds 62 .5% pass@4 with the w=64 window, vs. 56 .0% 

at w=16 in an earlier run; enlarging the exact window removes the only point where compressing decode history had cost quality. The result is decisive in one direction: streaming loses nothing 

relative to the accumulating cache, while replacing the accumulating cache’s O(g) exact tail (which grows with every one of the g generated tokens) with a bounded one; a large memory-efficiency gain on long generations at no quality cost. D.2 STREAMING -WINDOW ABLATION ON A LONG -GENERATION TASK

We begin with a controlled synthetic head that isolates the core effect. Table 8 is built so that

At matched rank we compare SVD on raw KV, which minimizes ∥K − Kr ∥F , against the attention-aware truncation of Eqs. (3) and (4).
