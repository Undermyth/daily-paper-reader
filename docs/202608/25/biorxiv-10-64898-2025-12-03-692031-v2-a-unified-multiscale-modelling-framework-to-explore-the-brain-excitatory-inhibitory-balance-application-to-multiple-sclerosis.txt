# Abstract 17 

Balanced excitation and inhibition are essential for brain dynamics , and their disruption can lead to network 18 

dysfunction in neurological diseases . Here , we present a conceptually uni$ied multiscale brain modelling 19 

framework combining Dynamic Causal Modelling (DCM ) applied to task and resting -state functional Magnetic 20 

Resonance Imaging (fMRI ) data and The Virtual Brain (TVB ) to characterise the excitatory /inhibitory balance of 21 

the brain . We applied the framework to a visuomotor brain subnetwork in a cohort of 9 healthy controls and 22 

17 people with multiple sclerosis (pwMS ). Acquired data included an event -related task fMRI experiment with 23 

variable grip force , resting -state fMRI , and diffusion -weighted imaging . The visuomotor network comprised the 24 

bilateral primary visual cortex (V1), left primary motor cortex (M1), supplementary motor and premotor cortex 25 

(SMAPMC ), cingulate cortex (CC ), superior parietal lobule (SPL ), and right cerebellar lobule VI (CR ). Results from 26 

DCM showed that while the overall network architecture was preserved in MS , there were signi$icant alterations 27 

in the excitatory /inhibitory nature of effective connectivity : at rest , a statistical change was observed in CR -to -28 

V1 connectivity , which was inhibitory in healthy volunteers but excitatory in MS . During task , effective 29 

connectivity feedback , including cerebellar self -connection , was positive in healthy volunteers but negative in 30 

MS and became increasingly dysregulated with higher motor demand . Alterations in functional and effective 31 

connectivity were associated with behavioural performance (task reaction time ) and clinical measures (disability 32 

severity ). At the overall group level , TVB parameters linked reduced NMDA -mediated excitatory gain to slower 33 

task responses . Moreover , integrating DCM and TVB demonstrated that higher global excitatory gain was 34 

associated with stronger task -engaged effective connectivity across sensorimotor and visuomotor pathways ,35 

linking network -level excitability captured by TVB to context -dependent recon$iguration of directed 36 

interactions and to connection -level strength revealed by DCM .37 

# 1. Introduction 38 

Balanced excitation and inhibition is a fundamental property of neuronal systems that supports stable activity 39 

across spatial scales . Disruptions of this balance can emerge locally , yet it can impact large -scale brain dynamics ,40 

in$luencing directed interactions between cortical and subcortical regions . Recent advances in computational 41 

neuroscience enable the integration of processes across micro -, meso -, and macroscale levels using 42 

complementary modelling approaches (De Schepper et al ., 2022; Geminiani et al ., 2018). At the mesoscale ,43

interactions (Geminiani et al ., 2024; Kennedy et al ., 2024), whereas at the macroscale , whole -brain activity 45 

inferred from functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI ) is typically modelled using neural mass or 46 

mean -$ield formulations constrained by region to region structural connectivity (Deco et al ., 2008; Kennedy et 47 

al ., 2024). Within this framework , the sign and strength of interregional interactions provide a parsimonious 48 

way of characterising network dynamics , but remain dif$icult to infer consistently across experimental contexts .49 

Dynamic Causal Modelling (DCM ) offers a principled generative framework for estimating effective connectivity ,50 

i.e. directed interactions between brain regions , from fMRI data (Friston et al ., 2003 a). In parallel , The Virtual 51 

Brain (TVB ) provides a platform to simulate whole -brain dynamics by combining structural connectivity with 52 

neural mass models parameterised in terms of excitatory and inhibitory synaptic gains (Ritter et al ., 2013; Sanz -53 

Leon et al ., 2013). While both approaches have been widely used , they are typically applied in isolation : DCM 54 

focuses on pairwise interactions estimated from observed data , whereas TVB emphasises global network 55 

behaviour emerging from generative simulations . The relationship between these two levels of description 56 

remains insuf$iciently characterised , particularly with respect to how local directed interactions relate to 57 

macroscopic network regimes .58 

Multiple Sclerosis (MS ) provides a relevant testbed to address this question . MS is a chronic neuroin$lammatory 59 

disease associated with widespread structural damage and functional reorganisation (Filippi et al ., 2019; 60 

Lassmann et al ., 2012; Ricciardi et al ., 2023; Rocca & Filippi , 2007). fMRI studies have consistently demonstrated 61 

altered network organisation during both rest and task (Alahmadi et al ., 2021; Alharthi & Almurdi , 2023; 62 

Mezzapesa et al ., 2008; Rocca et al ., 2012), including nonlinear modulation of visuomotor responses with 63 

increasing motor demand (Alahmadi et al ., 2017). However , these $indings are largely descriptive and do not 64 

provide direct insights into the underlying mechanisms governing interregional interactions . In particular , it 65 

remains unclear whether disease -related changes re$lect differences in the magnitude of coupling , changes in 66 

its directionality , or shifts in the inferred excitatory /inhibitory balance .67 

Computational approaches offer a means to address these limitations by estimating latent neuronal processes 68 

from measured signals (D’Angelo & Jirsa , 2022; Frässle et al ., 2018). DCM studies in MS have reported alterations 69 

in effective connectivity , often with substantial inter -subject variability (Au Duong et al ., 2005; Mansoory et al ., 70 

2022; Savini et al ., 2019 a). In parallel , a small number of studies have used TVB to infer global changes in network 71 

dynamics (Martı́ -Juan et al ., 2023; Sorrentino et al ., 2024). However , these approaches have not yet been 72 

combined to examine how connection -level estimates of effective connectivity relate to network -level 73 

parameters within a conceptually uni$ied modelling framework , nor how these relationships depend on 74 

behavioural context (e.g. task vs rest ). 75 

In this study , we address this gap by integrating DCM , spectral DCM (sp -DCM ), and TVB within a single modelling 76 

framework applied to a visuomotor network in people with MS (pwMS ) and healthy volunteers (HV ). We focus 77 

on three questions : ( i) whether the inferred sign (excitatory vs inhibitory ) of effective connectivity differs 78 

between groups ; ( ii ) whether these differences depend on context (task vs resting -state ); and (iii ) how 79 

connection -level estimates relate to global model parameters and behavioural or clinical measures . To this end ,80 

we analyse task -based fMRI , resting -state fMRI , and diffusion MRI from a previously acquired dataset (Alahmadi 81 

et al ., 2021), focusing on a visuomotor network including bilateral primary visual cortex (V1), left primary motor 82 

cortex (M1), supplementary motor and premotor cortex (SMAPMC ), cingulate cortex (CC ), superior parietal lobule 83 

(SPL ), and right cerebellar lobule VI (CR ) ( Lorenzi et al ., 2025). DCM and sp -DCM are used to estimate effective 84 

connectivity under task and resting conditions , respectively , while TVB is employed to model large -scale 85 

dynamics constrained by structural connectivity . The resulting parameters are then related to behavioural 86 

performance and clinical disability using Parametric Empirical Bayes (PEB ) ( Zeidman et al ., 2019) and regression 87 

model approaches .88

Participants 109 

Participants were selected from a cohort recruited as part of a previous study (Alahmadi et al ., 2021): nine right -110 

handed HV [4 females , 5 males ; age = 37 ± 6 years ], selected based on availability of resting -state fMRI , DWI ,111 

and task fMRI data ; and all fourteen right -handed pwMS (Relapsing -Remitting MS , RRMS ), with three additional 112

6.5)]. For the TVB analyses , a subset of these participants was used (7 HV and 14 pwMS ), based on the availability 114 

of the data necessary for model construction . Participants ’ handedness was evaluated using the Edinburgh 115

MRI Acquisition & fMRI Study Design 119 

MRI data were acquired for all participants using a 3 Tesla Philips Achieva scanner (Philips Healthcare , Best ,120 

The Netherlands ) equipped with a 32-channel receive -only head coil . All image volumes were aligned to the 121 

bicallosal line . The imaging protocol included structural , functional (task -based and resting -state ), and DWI 122 

acquisitions . Details are reported in Supplementary Table 1. 123 

Task -based fMRI data were collected using a BOLD -sensitive T2*-weighted echo -planar imaging sequence while 124 

participants performed an event -related power grip task (TR /TE = 2500/35 ms , $lip angle = 90 °, voxel size = 3 ×125 

3 × 2.7 mm³ ). Participants used their right (dominant ) hand to squeeze an fMRI -compatible rubber ball in 126 

response to a visual cue . The task consisted of 75 active trials distributed evenly and randomly across $ive GF 127 

levels (20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, and 60% of each participant ’s maximum voluntary contraction ). Actual task execution 128 

timings were recorded for each subject , and reaction times (delays between cue onset and motor execution )129 

were calculated and z-score transformed for subsequent analyses .130 

Resting -state fMRI data were acquired using the same imaging parameters as the task -based fMRI sequence ,131 

with a reduced number of volumes . Structural imaging included high -resolution T1-weighted and PD /T2-132 

weighted sequences for anatomical characterization and lesion detection .133 

DWI was performed using a high angular resolution diffusion imaging protocol . Axial -oblique DWI data were 134 

acquired with a cardiac -gated spin -echo (SE ) EPI sequence (TR ≈ 24,000 ms , TE = 68 ms , $lip angle = 90 °, voxel 135 

size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³ ). 136

Brain Modelling 209 

The brain modelling approach (Figure 1) comprises three pipelines : DCM , sp -DCM , and TVB , integrating 210 

structural , functional , and effective connectivity . Analyses were performed in HV and pwMS , with correlations 211 

between model parameters and clinical disability also examined . Details follow .212 

Dynamic Causal Modelling 213 

DCM analyses task fMRI data to model effective connectivity between pairs of regions (Zeidman , Jafarian , Corbin ,214 

et al ., 2019). V1 was set as the driving input according to the nature of the task and our previous work (Lorenzi 215 

et al ., 2025). DCM 12.5 was used as implemented in SPM 12 ( MATLAB 2022 b). 216 

Predicted neural dynamics were transformed into BOLD signals using the haemodynamic forward model 217 

(Friston et al ., 2000; Stephan et al ., 2007 b), to extract endogenous coupling between pairs of regions ,218 

independently of the context (i.e., $ixed (intrinsic ) effective connectivity ). After , the impact of the experimental 219 

stimuli (i.e. GF levels ) was modelled as a 1st order (linear ) or 2nd order (non -linear ) modulation on the strength 220 

of the $ixed effective connectivity between regions . Fixed effective connectivity was examined as the $irst step 221 

(S1) and modulations on $ixed effective connectivity as second step (S2). For both S1 and S2, the pipeline 222 

described by (Lorenzi et al ., 2025) for action execution during the GF task was followed , separately for HV and 223 

pwMS . In S1, the same previously published network architectures (Supplementary Figure 1) were tested .224 

Bayesian model selection (BMS ) was used to select the best -$itting model for a different set of hypotheses at a225 

group -level (Penny et al ., 2010). After identifying the best -$itting model for both HV and pwMS (S1), each model 226 

con$iguration was further investigated by estimating the posterior probability (Pp ) of each connection (S1) and 227 

modulation (S2) speci$ied in the model architecture . In S2 both 1st and 2nd order modulations were imposed on 228 

each pair of connections on the winning S1 model , focusing on the visuo -to -plan loop (V1-CR -SMAPMC -CC ); 229 

different combinations were tested following a stack procedure that selected the most probable modulation for 230 

a certain connection and moved along the loop (Supplementary Figure 2). The overall process quanti$ied the 231 

model likelihood , assessed as model evidence using the experimental fMRI time -series data . Then , Random 232 

Effects -BMS (RFX -BMS ) was employed to determine whether the winning model varied across subjects (Penny 233 

et al ., 2010). Bayesian Model Averaging (BMA ) was employed to integrate the statistical structure by computing 234 

a weighted average of each connectivity strength across the model space (Friston et al ., 2016). 235

The Virtual Brain Modelling 243 

The TVB (version 2.7.2) work$low , as reported in (Monteverdi et al ., 2022), was followed (Figure 1. B). The Wong -244 

Wang neural mass model (Deco et al ., 2014) was chosen to characterize local cortical activity through two 245 

coupled populations representing excitatory (glutamatergic ) and inhibitory (GABAergic ) neurons . This model is 246 

characterized by four parameters : i) the global coupling 

(G

) that helps quantifying the overall integration 247 

capacity of the brain and determines how strongly brain regions in$luence each other ; ii ) the NMDA synaptic 248 

weight (JNMDA ) that determines the strength of the excitatory (glutamatergic ) synaptic connections ; iii ) the 249 

inhibitory coupling parameter (Ji) that controls the strength of inhibitory (GABAergic ) synaptic connections ; and 250 

iv ) the recurrent excitatory strength of each brain region (w+) that shows how strongly excitatory populations 251 

excite themselves .252 

The SC was used to connect all the nodes of the visuomotor network and each node was associated with a253 

Wong -Wang model (Wong et al ., 2006). Then , TVB generated a simulated FC and FCD for different combinations 254 

of model parameters (G, JNMDA , Ji, w+) that were optimized using an iterative $itting approach described in (Good 255 

et al ., 2022): $irst the PCC between simulated and experimental FC was maximized , then Kolmogorov -Smirnov 256 

(KS ) distance between simulated and experimental FCD was minimized . This optimisation was run for all 257 

participants to the study , obtaining a set of subject -speci$ic model parameters for the network .258 

Statistical Analysis and Disease Sensitivity 259 

An independent sample t-test was conducted to compare the best -$itting S1 model effective connectivity 260 

matrices between the HV and pwMS for both DCM and sp -DCM using R (version 2024.12.0). P-values were 261 

corrected for multiple comparisons using the false discovery rate (FDR ) and the signi$icance threshold was set 262 

at 0.05. 263 

PEB framework was used for group -level DCM analysis with clinical /behavioural scores (Friston et al ., 2015; 264 

Friston et al ., 2016). While DCM provides valuable estimates of subject -speci$ic effective connectivity that can 265 

differ between groups , understanding the clinical relevance of such differences requires an additional step that 266 

can be achieved by applying PEB analysis ; providing a hierarchical Bayesian approach that accounts for 267 

between -subjects ’ effects , identi$ies connectivity alterations across individuals and characterises how 268 

experimental or clinical variables are associated with effective connectivity differences . All statistical analysis 269 

assumed that the reaction time measured from the GF task performance can be considered a behavioural test 270 

of functional integrity for all subjects , while EDSS was investigated considering only pwMS . PEB was applied 271 

across all subjects : ( i) to investigate group -level effects of the GF task performance , assessed through the z-score 272 

transformed reaction time , and (ii ) only to pwMS for EDSS to examine the association of disease severity with 273 

the effective connectivity between speci$ic nodes . After , the same analysis was repeated for sp -DCM .274

Akaike Information Criterion (AIC ) as the selection criterion . Bidirectional stepwise procedures were performed 278 

independently to ensure the robustness of the predictors ’ selection . A p-value threshold of 0.05 was used to 279 

determine statistical signi$icance for a variable inclusion in the $inal model . A post

# 4. Discussion 437 

Our principal $inding is that DCM , spDCM , and TVB can be brought together within a uni$ied modelling 438 

framework that links effective connectivity to global -network dynamics . By linking connection -level estimates 439 

to emergent network dynamics , this approach allowed us to characterize differences between HV and pwMS in 440 

the visuomotor network and to explore the inferred excitatory -inhibitory balance underlying interregional 441 

interactions .442 

Relationship between connection 

- level and network 

- level descriptions 443 

A central observation is that robust differences between HV and pwMS emerged at the level of pairwise effective 444 

connectivity , while global TVB parameters did not show signi$icant group differences . This apparent 445 

dissociation highlights an important methodological point : alterations in directed interactions between regions 446 

do not necessarily translate into measurable shifts in global network parameters , at least within the constraints 447 

of the present model and sample size .448 

From a modelling perspective , DCM and TVB provide complementary but non -equivalent descriptions of brain 449 

dynamics . DCM estimates context -dependent , directed coupling between prede$ined regions based on observed 450 

fMRI signals (Friston et al ., 2003), while TVB simulates emergent network dynamics from structural connectivity 451 

and biophysically interpretable parameters (Ritter et al ., 2013; Sanz -Leon et al ., 2013). The present results 452 

suggest that local recon$igurations of effective connectivity may occur without consistent shifts in global 453 

dynamical regimes , or that such shifts are more dif$icult to detect given current model parameterisations . This 454

Context - dependent modulation of effective connectivity 457 

Across both groups , effective connectivity differed between task and resting -state conditions , con$irming that 458 

interregional interactions are strongly context dependent . In HV , resting -state dynamics were characterised by 459 

predominantly inhibitory interactions , whereas task engagement was associated with a shift towards excitatory 460 

in$luences within the visuomotor network . This pattern is consistent with the interpretation of resting -state 461 

activity as a constrained regime that limits excessive signal propagation while maintaining readiness for task 462 

engagement .463 

In pwMS , this context -dependent modulation was altered . In particular , several connections showed reversals 464 

in the inferred sign (excitatory vs inhibitory ) between groups , notably within cerebellar -cortical pathways .465 

These $indings extend previous fMRI studies demonstrating altered task -related responses and nonlinear 466 

scaling with motor demand in MS (Alahmadi et al ., 2017, 2021) by suggesting that such differences may re$lect 467 

changes in the organisation of directed interactions rather than solely in regional activation amplitudes .468 

Cerebellar 

- cortical interactions as a locus of reconCiguration 469 

The most consistent differences between groups involved cerebellar -cortical interactions , particularly the 470 

connection from cerebellar lobule VI (CR ) to V1. During rest , this connection was inferred as inhibitory in HV 471 

but excitatory in pwMS , indicating a qualitative change in the direction of in$luence . Additional alterations in 472 

task -dependent modulations further implicated the cerebellum , including differences in the sign of feedback 473 

pathways and self -connections .474 

These $indings are consistent with prior work highlighting the role of the cerebellum in coordinating 475 

visuomotor processing and adapting motor outputs to task demands (Diedrichsen et al ., 2007; Lorenzi et al ., 476 

2025; Wolpert et al ., 1998). In the context of MS , cerebellar involvement has been associated with both structural 477 

damage and functional reorganisation (Dogonowski et al ., 2013; Savini et al ., 2019). The present results extend 478 

these observations by suggesting that cerebellar contributions may be altered at the level of directed 479 

interactions , potentially in$luencing how sensory information is integrated into motor planning .480 

Previous studies have linked glutamatergic and GABAergic mechanisms to functional changes in MS within 481 

sensorimotor and parietal regions . Reduced glutamate has been associated with demyelination , while elevated 482 

GABA has been reported in sensorimotor areas , consistent with cortical reorganisation during disease 483 

progression (Bhattacharyya et al ., 2013; Cawley et al ., 2015; Macmillan et al ., 2016; Nantes et al ., 2017; Orije et 484 

al ., 2015.) While DCM does not measure neurotransmitter concentrations directly , alterations in the sign and 485 

strength of effective connectivity may provide model -based evidence of such imbalance . The present $indings 486 

are consistent with this picture , suggesting that disease -related shifts in excitatory and inhibitory coupling 487 

within the visuomotor network may re$lect underlying neurochemical alterations , the temporal evolution and 488 

clinical relevance of which warrant further investigation .489 

Associations with behaviour and clinical measures 490 

Both DCM -derived effective connectivity and TVB parameters showed associations with behavioural 491 

performance and clinical disability . In particular , reaction time was related to the strength and sign of multiple 492 

connections , as well as to the NMDA -mediated excitatory gain parameter (JNMDA ) in TVB . Participants with lower 493 

excitatory gain exhibited slower responses , suggesting that reduced effective integration within the network is 494 

associated with decreased behavioural ef$iciency .495

Linking connection 

- level effective connectivity to network 

- level dynamics 503 

A key insight from the combined DCM -TVB analysis is the relationship between global excitatory gain (JNMDA )504 

and context -dependent recon$iguration of effective connectivity . JNMDA values , obtained by optimising the 505 

excitatory synaptic gain at the network level , explained effective connectivity values across speci$ic pathways 506 

during task , including from CR to V1 and projections from SMAPMC to M1 and CC . Within the constraints of the 507 

models used , this pattern suggests that greater baseline excitatory drive may require stronger directed coupling 508 

during task execution to sustain effective sensorimotor integration , particularly along motor planning (i.e.509 

SMAPMC to M1), visuomotor integration and monitoring (SMAPMC to CC , M1 to SPL ) pathways , as well as 510 

cerebellar engagement from early visual regions (CR to V1). 511 

In contrast , during resting -state , JNMDA was associated with a more differentiated pattern : a reduced interaction 512 

from distributed sensorimotor regions (M1 and CR ) to SMAPMC , alongside increased coupling among 513 

visuomotor pairs (e.g., V1-M1, SMAPMC -CC ). This suggests that , in the absence of task demands , network 514 

dynamics may favour intrinsic visuomotor coupling and internal evaluation processes , with relatively weaker 515 

integration of distributed sensorimotor inputs into motor planning and control hubs . The increased SMAPMC 516 

to CC interaction at rest may therefore re$lect a greater coupling between action planning and evaluative 517 

systems , consistent with ongoing monitoring or readiness processes in the absence of overt behaviour .518 

Notably , cerebellar interactions showed no signi$icant associations in the resting -state condition , in contrast to 519 

their engagement during task , consistent with the reduced need for online sensory feedback and error 520 

correction when no task is being performed . Together , these $indings highlight that DCM -derived effective 521 

connectivity captures context -speci$ic reweighting of directed interactions , while TVB parameters such as JNMDA 522 

provide a more global constraint on network excitability . Their integration suggests that global synaptic gain 523 

does not directly determine network behaviour but modulates the extent to which directed interactions must 524 

be engaged to meet task demands , particularly in pathways supporting sensorimotor integration and 525 

performance monitoring .526 

Study Considerations & Future Directions 527 

Several limitations should be considered . First , the sample size is relatively small , which limits statistical power 528 

and the generalisability of the $indings . Second , the analysis focused on a prede$ined visuomotor network ,529 

potentially excluding regions that may also contribute to disease -related alterations . Given that the dentate 530 

nucleus represents the principal cerebellar output channel projecting to the cortical areas via synapses in the 531 

thalamus and basal ganglia (Dum & Strick , 2003; Hoshi et al ., 2005), future studies should investigate their 532 

involvement to better characterize the pathways mediating cerebro -cerebellar dysfunction in MS . Third , the 533 

interpretation of excitatory and inhibitory interactions relies on model -based inference and cannot be directly 534 

equated with synaptic or neurotransmitter -level processes . Finally , the integration between DCM and TVB 535 

remains mainly conceptual , apart from the $inal PEB analysis , and further methodological work is needed to 536 

establish formal links between these frameworks .537

# 5. Conclusion 538 

In summary , by combining DCM , sp -DCM , and TVB within a single conceptual framework , this study illustrates 539 

the potential of integrating complementary modelling approaches to characterise brain dynamics across scales .540 

The results in the small HV and MS study suggest that connection -level and network -level descriptions capture 541 

different aspects of disease -related alterations , and that combining them may provide a more complete account 542 

than either approach alone . We show that MS is associated with alterations of effective connectivity that are 543 

context -dependent and that may imply a change in the excitatory /inhibitory nature of network dynamics ,544 

particularly involving cerebellar -cortical interactions . These changes are detectable at the level of pairwise 545 

directed interactions but do not necessarily manifest as global shifts in network parameters . At group level ,546 

integrating DCM and TVB can indeed link network -level excitability to context -dependent recon$iguration of 547 

directed interactions . This opens new avenue to study brain dynamics and neurological conditions .548
