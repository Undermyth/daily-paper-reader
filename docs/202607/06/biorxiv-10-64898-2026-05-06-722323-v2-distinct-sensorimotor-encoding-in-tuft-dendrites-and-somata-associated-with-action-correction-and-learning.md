## 摘要
额叶皮层在动作控制和运动技能学习中起着关键作用。在额叶皮层第5层（L5）神经元的第1层顶端丛状树突中，精确的输入模式和反向传播的动作电位可以触发强大的再生事件，这对于灵活的计算和学习可能至关重要。然而，尚不清楚额叶皮层L5回路中的丛状活动是否编码了与其向下游目标输出的信息不同的感觉运动信息。利用纵向双光子钙成像，我们研究了小鼠在学习对提示的灵巧动作进行离散变化期间，额叶皮层L5端脑外（ET）神经元顶端丛状树突和胞体中的感觉运动编码。在学习过程中，运动错误要么触发纠正动作，要么不触发，这使我们能够将错误信号与选择性针对纠正动作的信号区分开来。胞体活动追踪了提示指令和动作，而丛状活动主要追踪提示指令。学习过程中的运动错误揭示了额外独特的丛状活动，这些活动与纠正动作选择性相关。此外，与胞体相比，学习诱导了丛状树突响应增益和净选择性的不同变化。我们的测量揭示了丛状树突和胞体在感觉运动选择性、对纠正动作的敏感性和功能可塑性方面的系统性差异，为研究树突计算对运动技能学习的贡献奠定了基础。

## Abstract
Frontal cortex plays critical roles in action control and motor skill learning. Within the layer 1 apical tuft dendrites of layer 5 (L5) neurons in frontal cortex, precise input patterns and back-propagating action potentials can trigger powerful regenerative events that may be essential for flexible computation and learning. However, it remains unclear whether tuft activity in frontal cortical L5 circuits encodes sensorimotor information that differs from the information conveyed by their outputs to downstream targets. Using longitudinal two-photon calcium imaging, we investigated sensorimotor encoding in the apical tuft dendrites and somata of L5 extratelencephalic (ET) neurons in the frontal cortex of mice during learning of a discrete change to a cued dexterous action. During learning, movement errors either triggered corrective action or did not, allowing us to dissociate error signals from signals selective for corrective action. Somatic activity tracked both instructional cues and action, whereas tuft activity predominantly tracked instructional cues. Movement errors during learning revealed additional distinct tuft activity that was selectively associated with corrective actions. Furthermore, learning induced divergent changes in the response gain and net selectivity of tuft dendrites compared to somata. Our measurements uncover systematic differences between the tuft dendrites and somata in sensorimotor selectivity, sensitivity to corrective action, and functional plasticity, providing a foundation for investigating the contributions of dendritic computation to motor skill learning.
