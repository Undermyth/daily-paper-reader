## 摘要
神经群体的活动通常编码关于感觉或运动变量的信息，这些信息超出了通过点估计所能捕获的范围。我们提出并比较了两种量化这种额外或辅助信息及其与不确定性关系的方法：活动与估计误差之间的互信息，以及费希尔信息损失（可在信息几何中解释为曲率）。我们表明，估计误差偏离高斯性（包括人类行为任务中经常观察到的长尾分布）是辅助信息存在的预期推论。然而，具有相似估计误差分布的群体在辅助信息内容上可能有很大差异，这取决于噪声特性。对于给定的群体调谐和噪声模型，我们的结果量化了仅从群体活动中可获得的不确定性信息的上限：超出此上限的行为表明访问了关于不确定性的独立信息来源。最后，我们对比了外部噪声和内部信号强度减弱对辅助信息及误差高斯性的影响。我们的工作直接将关于不确定性的知识与感觉估计的非高斯性联系起来，并为研究神经群体活动中元认知的基础建立了连贯的理论框架。

作者总结：大脑通过固有噪声的神经活动处理关于外部世界的感觉证据。因此，行为判断（如估计移动物体的方向）本质上是不确定的。尽管包括人类在内的动物通常利用不确定性在风险下指导决策，但神经群体如何表示这种不确定性仍不清楚。在这项工作中，我们展示了用于解码感觉变量的相同神经活动如何也能提供关于估计可靠性的信息。我们引入了一个数学框架，直接从神经群体的编码模型中量化这种“辅助信息”。我们证明辅助信息能预测估计误差的非高斯性，并设定元认知敏感性的上限（主观置信度跟踪性能的准确度）。关键的是，我们表明具有不同噪声特征的神经群体可以产生几乎相同的估计误差，同时提供非常不同程度的不确定性信息。这突显了在比较感觉编码的竞争模型时，评估辅助信息（而不仅仅是误差模式）的重要性。

## Abstract
The activity of neural populations typically encodes more information about sensory or motor variables than can be captured by point estimates of the variables. We present and compare two approaches to quantifying this additional or ancillary information and its relationship to uncertainty: the mutual information between activity and estimation error, and the Fisher information loss, which can be interpreted in terms of curvature in information geometry. We show that deviations from Gaussianity of estimation errors, including the long tails frequently observed in human behavioural tasks, are an expected corollary of the presence of ancillary information. However, populations with similar distributions of estimation error can differ substantially in their ancillary information content depending on the noise characteristics. For a given population tuning and noise model, our results quantify an upper bound on the information about uncertainty that can be obtained from population activity alone: behaviour demonstrating knowledge in excess of this bound would indicate access to a separate source of information about uncertainty. Finally, we contrast the effects of external noise and decreasing internal signal strength on ancillary information and the Gaussianity of errors. Our work directly relates knowledge about uncertainty to non-Gaussianity in sensory estimates, and establishes a coherent theoretical foundation for investigating the basis of metacognition in neural population activity.

Author summaryThe brain processes sensory evidence about the external world via inherently noisy neural activity. As a result, behavioural judgments - such as estimating the direction of a moving object - are fundamentally uncertain. While animals, including humans, routinely use uncertainty to guide decisions under risk, how neural populations represent this uncertainty remains unclear. In this work, we show how the same neural activity used to decode a sensory variable can also provide information about the estimates reliability. We introduce a mathematical framework to quantify this "ancillary information" directly from a neural populations encoding model. We demonstrate that ancillary information predicts non-Gaussianity in estimation errors and sets an upper bound on metacognitive sensitivity (how accurately subjective confidence tracks performance). Crucially, we show that neural populations with distinct noise characteristics can yield near-identical estimation errors while providing very different degrees of uncertainty information. This highlights the importance of evaluating ancillary information, not just error patterns, when comparing competing models of sensory coding.
