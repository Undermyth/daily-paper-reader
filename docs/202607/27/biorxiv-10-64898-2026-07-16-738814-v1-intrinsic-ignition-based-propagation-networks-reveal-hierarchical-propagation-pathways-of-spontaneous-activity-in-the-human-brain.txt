Abstract  (24 2 words) 19 

Directed communication across  brain  regions is fundamental to human brain function,  yet how local 20 

spontaneous activity  links  to directed whole -brain propagation patterns remains elusive . Prior  studies 21 

have  characterized local propagation events  and  global wave -like dynamics,  but  a framework linking 22 

local events , interregional propagation pathways , and  canonical large -scale propagation patterns remains 23 

lacking.  Using resting -state functional MRI data from the Human Connectome Project 7T cohort and an 24 

intrinsic ignition framework, we quantified cascades of suprathreshold activity events  and  construct ed 25 

directed propagation probability networks. We found spatially heterogeneous regional propagation 26 

preferences, with high outgoing propagation  preferences  predominantly localized in somatomotor, visual, 27 

and default -mode regions. At the  functional  system level, propagation modules broadly separated visual, 28 

somatomotor, dorsal attention, and ventral attention networks from association systems. Regional 29 

stepwise propagation pathways were aligned with the principal functional gradient, and  greater 30 

hierarchical separation between regions  was associated  with  longer  propagation distance. These 31 

pathways  formed two canonical propagation patterns linking primary and association systems, with 32 

somatomotor regions preferentially  initiated  in bottom -up propagation, default -mode regions in top -33 

down propagation, and attention networks occupying intermediate positions. Notably, the two patterns 34 

were not simple mirror  images , with top -down propagation showing  substantial  deviations within 35 

somatomotor and visual systems. These findings were replicated in an independent cohort. Moreover, 36 

individual propagation architecture predicted cognitive performance and tobacco -use behavior. 37 

Collectively, our  findings  provide  cross -scale  insights into  spontaneous activity  propagation  in the human 38 

brain by  bridging  local ignition events with  hierarchical  whole -brain propagation p athways  and 39 

behaviorally relevant individual differences. 40

Significance 42 

Spontaneous brain activity is often  quantified by the temporal synchronization between regions , but this 43 

approach  does not show how  local  activity propagate s across the brain. We used an interpretable ignition 44 

framework to link local spontaneous events and their cascades to directed whole -brain propagation 45 

patterns. This approach revealed  out going  propagation  preferences  of primary and default -mode regions ,46 

a modular  architecture  separating primary and attentional systems from association systems, and two 47 

canonical propagation patterns aligned with the brain’s functional hierarchy. These  two  patterns were not 48 

simple mirror images, indicating  partially distinct  bottom -up and top -down routes. Individual 49 

propagation architecture also predicted cognitive performance and tobacco -use behavior. These findings 50 

provide a path -based view of spontaneous brain dynamics. 51

Introduction 53 

Brain function  depend s on  coordinated  interactions and efficient  communication s among  distributed 54 

brain region s (1 -4) . Neuroimag ing -based f unctional connectomics has revealed key principles of  human 55 

brain network organization by quantifying interregional synchrony  in spontaneous activity  (5, 6) . The 56 

functional  connectome exhibits non -trivial topological properties,  including  small -world  properties  (7 -57 

9) , modular architecture  (10 -12) , and highly connected  hubs  (13 -15) . More recently,  gradient -based 58 

analyses  have revealed  a principal  axis  of functional  connectivity  organization , with primary 59 

sensor imotor  systems  and default -mode systems locat ed  at opposite  ends  (16 -18) . This  axis has been 60 

linked  to  hierarch ical  information processing , ranging  from  sensory  percept ion to multimodal integration 61 

and abstract cognition  (16 -18) . It  has  also  provide d valuable insights  into  individual  cognition and 62 

behavior  (16) , as well as  alterations associated with  brain  development  (19 -21)  and  neuropsychiatric 63 

disorders  (22, 23) . However,  this  synchrony -based  gradient  primarily  capture s a static spatial  layout 64 

rather than  the  temporal  propagation  of activity  across  regions . How  spontaneous activity  propagat es 65 

across  the  brain  and how  th e dynamics  relate s to  the  macroscale functional hierar chy  remain  to be 66 

elucidated .67 

Accumulating  resting -state functional magnetic resonance imaging  (R -fMRI)  studies  indicate  that 68 

spontaneous brain activity  exhibits  structured  propagation  pattern s, including  systematic time -lag 69 

relationships across regions  (24, 25) , event -trig gered cascades  (26, 27) , and  wave -like spreading  (28 -31) .70 

Such propagation dynamics  var ies  with  perceptual and cognitive demands  (28, 32) , developmental stages 71 

(33, 34) , and brain disorders  (35, 36) , suggesting  that  directed activity propagation may play an important 72 

role  in supporting healthy brain function . Importantly,  recent studies  have  fu rther  shown  that  large -scale 73 

activity propagation  can be summarized  by  a small  number  of dominant spatiotemporal  components ,74 

such as  sensory  to  association propagation , task -positive  to  default -mode propagation,  and somatomotor 75 

to  visual propagation  (29, 30, 34, 37) . However,  the  local and global descriptions of spontaneous 76 

propagation  remain  insufficiently integrated . Local analyses typically  focus on  event -level cascades, 77 

pairwise delays, or local trajectories  (25, 28) , whereas  global  analyses  often  characterize  brain -wide 78 

propagation morphology or low -dimensional spatiotemporal modes  (29, 30) . It remains unknown how 79 

local propagation events are routed through specific interregional pathways to  give rise to  canonical 80 

brain -wide propagation patterns. 81 

To address this  gap , we  employed  an  interpretable  intrinsic ignition  framework  (38, 39)  to construct 82 

directed  propagation probability networks  from  spontaneous high -amplitude activity events . Here, we 83 

used  R-fMRI  data  from 157 healthy adults  from the Human Connectome Project 7T  (HCP -7T)  (40, 41) ,84 

and  the high spatial resolution of the 7T data is particularly suited to resolving  local activity events . By 85 

transforming local ignition -event cascades into directed region -to -region propagation probability 86 

networks, this approach allowed us to quantify fine -grained interregional propagation pathways and their 87 

organization into system -level and whole -brain patterns.  We  characterize d regional propagation 88 

preferences,  system -level propagation architecture,  stepwise  propagation  pathways , and canonical 89

Results 95 

We  characterized brain -wide  activity  propagation  based on  the intrinsic ignition framework  (38, 39) ,96 

which assumes th at  high -amplitude  ignition events  propagate to other regions within a limited temporal 97 

window, giving rise to spatiotemporal cascades (Fig. 1A).  The cerebral cortex was parcellated into  1,000 98 

cortical regions of interest  using  the Schaefer 1000 -parcel functional atlas  (42) . After excluding two 99 

parcels without valid BOLD time  courses , 998 valid cortical nodes were retained for subsequent analyses. 100 

Regional  ignition events were defined as  upward  threshold -crossing events  in  the corresponding  regional 101 

blood oxygenation -level -dependent (BOLD)  time course  (Fig. 1B) . The propagation  of  these  ignition 102 

events  was quantified with  an  interregional  propagation probability matrix, in which each element,  pij ,103 

quantified the likelihood that an ignition event in node  i was followed by a subsequent event in node  j104 

(Fig. 1C) . S ee  Supplementary  Methods  for details .105

To  delineate  the fine -grained routes through which ignition events propagate across the cortex , we 130 

derived  a directed binary  propagation  network by retaining  strong  edges  in the propagation probability 131 

matrix  (pth  > 0.2)  (Fig.  S2 ; see  Supplementary  Methods ). For each region, we calculated the  stepwise 132 

shortest -path  distance s, referred  to  as  propagation  steps , from  this  region to all other  regions  (Fig. 3A). 133 

We first assessed whether t he  overall  layout of  each region’s  stepwise  propagation  pathway  was  aligned 134 

with the  cortical hierarchy  captured  by the principal functional gradient  (see Methods) . For each region, 135 

we  computed  Spearman ’s  correlation between its  propagation -step  distances to  all target  regions and  the 136 

corresponding  principal functional gradient  scores  (Fig. 3B).  Regions with p ositive correlations were 137 

primarily located in primary sensorimotor  and visual  regions  as well as the  superior parietal lobule ,138 

whereas  regions with  negative correlations were  primarily located  in  the medial and lateral frontal 139 

cort ices , lateral temporal cortex, angular gyrus, and posterior cingulate cortex/precuneus  (Fig. 3B). These 140 

correlations were bimodally distributed (Fig. 3C , Hartigan’s dip test , dip = 0.07 38 , p < 0.001) , with  strong 141 

separation between the two  modes  (Ashman’s  D = 4.8 0, see  Supplementary  Methods ).  At the  functional 142 

system -level,  positive correlations in  the  somatomotor, dorsal attention, and visual networks  indicate d143 

stepwise  propagation profile s that follow the  sensory -to -association axis, whereas negative correlations 144 

in the default -mode, frontoparietal, and limbic networks  indicate d propagation  profiles oriented in the 145 

opposite direction . Notably, the ventral attention network exhibited near -zero median  correlations ,146 

suggesting  heterogeneous or less consistently gradient -aligned  propagation profiles. 147

Discussion 205 

Using  R-fMRI  data from two independent datasets , we  constructed  propagation probability networks  and 206 

stepwise propagation pathways to  characterize  the  interregional  propagation  of  spontaneous  brain activity .207 

Extending  beyond  previous studies that primarily characterized brain -wide propagation patterns  (29, 33, 208 

34, 37) , we examined fine -grained propagation profiles at regional and  functional  system levels.  We 209 

found that t he propagation  probability matrix  exhibited  evident  asymmetry and spatially heterogeneous 210 

regional preferences.  At the  functional  system  level , the  propagation skeleton exhibited  modular 211 

organization , broadly separating  primary  perceptual  and attention systems from  association systems .212 

Region -wise  stepwise propagation pathways  were  systematically  organized along  the principal 213 

functional gradient , forming  two canonical  patterns  in which  propagation distances increas ed  with 214 

hierarchical separation . Finally,  individual propagation probability  matrices  predict ed  individual 215 

cognitive  and behavioral  performance.  Together,  these findings  reveal  structured, directed propagation 216 

pathways that link local ignition events to hierarchical whole -brain propagation patterns and behaviorally 217 

relevant individual differences. 218 

By leverag ing  the intrinsic ignition framework  (38, 39) , we estimated  directed,  interregional propagation 219 

probability  matrices . This approach provides a network -level representation of spontaneous activity 220 

propagation,  linking  local  high -amplitude  event s to  system -level  propagation architecture  and gradient -221 

aligned stepwise pathways . In this sense, the present framework complements prior global descriptions 222 

of propagating waves , such  as  time -lag threads , brain -wide wave morpholog ies , and  low -dimensional 223 

spatiotemporal  modes  (25, 29, 30, 33) , by specifying which regions preferentially transmit or receive 224 

activity and how propagation may proceed through intermediate regions and functional systems .225 

The  propagation probabilit y matrices exhibited robust asymmetry, indicating that spontaneous activity 226 

propagation is directionally organized rather than reducible to undirected co -fluctuation s. This 227 

asymmetry  is  consistent  with  the broader notion that  communication in brain networks  can be 228 

directionally biased and spatially heterogeneous  (44) , although the present analysis focuses on  BOLD -229 

dependent  propagation rather than direct  measurements of  neural information transfer . Brain regions 230 

exhibited  heterogeneous  in coming  and  outgoing  preferences  even after correcting for local ignition  event 231 

frequency, suggesting that propagation  preferences  are not simply  attributable to regional differences in 232 

ignition event occurrence.  Higher  out going  preferences  in the somatomotor and visual networks suggest 233 

that  primary perceptual systems may serve  as  frequent  sources of  spontaneous  activity  cascades, 234 

potentially reflecting the broadcast  of  perceptual or sensorimotor signals  (3, 45) . In contrast , higher 235 

incoming  preferences in  bilateral frontal, parietal, and temporal cortices suggest  that the association 236 

regions may act  as  convergence targets  for distributed activity , consistent with their roles in  multimodal 237

indicate s that individual cognitive performance  may  depend on how perceptual and action -related 298 

representations are dynamically integrated with attentional and association systems. Notably, for the 299 

frontoparietal system, the relatively higher predictive weights for incoming propagation than for outgoing 300 

propagation may suggest that frontoparietal contributions to cognitive performance are expressed less as 301 

a primary source of spontaneous propagation and more as a convergence or integration stage that receives 302 

attentional signals. 303 

Predictive features for tobacco -use  performances  were primarily  related to  propagation  within the 304 

somatomotor  network , consistent with  previous studies  suggesting that somatomotor and motor -related 305 

regions are involved in nicotine dependence and habitual smoking behaviors  (63 -65) . These regions have 306 

been repeatedly implicated in habit formation and cue -action coupling, reflecting a shift from goal -307 

directed to automatic behavioral control  (66) . Additionally, we found that high predictive weights were 308 

associated with propagation from the somatomotor network to the ventral attention and frontoparietal 309 

networks.  The v entral attention  network and insular regions are  known to support salience detection, 310 

interoceptive processing, and stimulus -driven reorienting  (52, 61) , whereas frontoparietal systems 311 

contribute to executive control and goal -directed regulation  (67, 68) . Our findings further indicate that 312

Several issues  warrant further consideration . First,  propagation  probability  was  estimat ed  from  BOLD 316 

signal s and it  may be affected  by  interregional  differences  in  hemodynamic  responses  (69) . Previous 317 

electrophysiological and multimodal studies have provided important evidence that large -scale fMRI 318 

propagation patterns have a neural basis  (25, 30) . Our  main findings  were  robust  to  correct ions  for 319 

regional  hemodynamic  latenc ies , further supporting the stability of the  observed  pro pagation 320 

organization . Future work should validate these pathways  using  imaging modalities  that more  direct ly 321 

measure neural activity  (3, 70) . Second, the limited temporal resolution of  fMRI  constrains the timescale 322 

at which propagation can be resolved. Higher  temporal  resolution recordings , such  as 323 

magnetoencephalography ( MEG ),  electroencephalography ( EEG) , or intracranial electrophysiology, 324 

may help characterize propagation  at  finer timescales  (71, 72) . Third , dynamic propagation  likely 325 

depends on the underlying structur al  architecture  (73, 74) . It remains unclear h ow  white matter 326 

connectivity and local  microstructure  constrain  direct ional  propagation  preferences, modular propagation 327 

organization, and  hierarchical routing . Fourth, we  focused on  resting -state  activity  propagation  in healthy 328 

adult s. Extending this framework to task conditions, developmental cohorts, and psychiatric populations 329 

may  clarify  how  propagation pathways  var y with cognitive  demand , maturation , and pathology.  Finally, 330 

stepwise shortest paths provide an interpretable representation of propagation routes, but they should be 331 

viewed as network -derived pathway estimates rather than direct observations of physical signal 332 

transmission. Future studies combining perturbation, longitudinal designs, and multimodal recordings 333 

will be important for testing the physiological interpretation of these pathways. 334 

Materials and Methods 335 

Neuroimaging data and participants.  Neuroimaging data were obtained from two resting -state fMRI 336 

datasets . The Human Connectome Project 7T dataset (HCP -7T; S1200 release)  (40, 41)  was used  as the 337 

discovery cohort and the Midnight Scan Club (MSC)  (75)  dataset as an independent validation cohort. 338 

Both datasets were obtained through established data -sharing repositories . For the HCP -7T dataset,  the 339 

original study procedures were approved by the  Institutional Review Board  of  Washington University  in 340 

accordance with the  original  study protocols . The MSC dataset was collected under the ethical approvals 341 

and informed -consent procedures of the original study.  Data from 1 57  healthy young adults  (22.0 -36.0 342 

years, 66 males)  were selected from t he HCP -7T  dataset.  These participants  complete d four  16 -min 343 

resting -state  runs  (64 min  in total ) and met  the HCP quality -assurance  criteria . The MSC dataset included 344 

densely sampled resting -state fMRI data from  ten  healthy adults . One participant was excluded  because 345 

of  excessive head motion and sleep during scanning,  leaving  nine participants for validation analyses. 346

and cluster solutions were evaluated using complementary validity criteria.  Further  methodological 357

from individual propagation probability networks. Statistical significance was assessed using 361
