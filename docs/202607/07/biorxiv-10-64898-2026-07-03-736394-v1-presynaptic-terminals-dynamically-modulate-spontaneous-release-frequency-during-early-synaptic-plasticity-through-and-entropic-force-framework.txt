Abstract: 14 

Spontaneous synaptic transmission has been established as essential for the maintenance of 15 

synaptic weights during action potential-induced transmission. However, spontaneous 16 

transmission also changes during synaptic plasticity and has been shown to, in part, mediate 17 

changes in synaptic weights. Despite decades of research, a coherent framework for 18 

understanding the complex molecular processes that support presynaptic spontaneous 19 

transmission during maintenance and plasticity has remained elusive. We show here that 20 

presynapses modulate spontaneous transmission frequency during the early time-course of 21 

plasticity following entropic force theory. We use live primary hippocampal cultures as a model 22 

system and induce plasticity using an established Long-Term Potentiation (LTP) protocol. We 23 

then use a combination of electron microscopy, fluorescence microscopy, and computational 24 

modeling to show how spontaneous release frequency dynamically changes during early 25 

plasticity. We use our entropic force theory to show how the dynamically changing synaptic 26 

vesicle pool structure mediates spontaneous release changes. Lastly, we show how these 27 

changes are altered in the presence of P301L tau leading to degeneration. The results from this 28 

study provide new insights that not only help understand normal synaptic function but also aid in 29 

understanding neurodegeneration. 30 

Introduction: 31 

Synapses represent the fundamental unit of information transfer in the central nervous 32 

system. They accomplish this task at the molecular level via an action potential-mediated 33 

release of neurotransmitter (NT) from a presynapse into the synaptic cleft, where the NT binds 34 

to postsynaptic receptors, initiating an ionic current (which we will call stimulated release). 1–5 

> 35

The complex molecular interactions that underlie this process are governed by a well-36 

established, experimentally supported multinomial model. 6–8 This multinomial model has also 37 

served as a basis for understanding changes in neuronal function under normal and 38

A major limitation with the multinomial model is the so-called stability/plasticity balance, 41 

which is the competition between maintaining (stability) and modifying (plasticity) synaptic 42 

weights. 13,14 This problem has been difficult to solve because of the complex combination of 43 

molecular-level changes that occur both in the presynapse 15–17 and postsynapse 18–21 between 44 

stimulated release events. Thus, over the last couple of decades, the role of changing synaptic 45 

behavior between stimulated release events has become a major focus. 46 

One long-hypothesized pathway supporting synaptic maintenance has been 47 

spontaneous synaptic transmission. 22–24 In the absence of an action potential, presynapses will 48 

spontaneously release NT at a regular frequency. 23,25–27 Despite the fact that spontaneous 49 

release uses similar molecular machinery (such as synaptic vesicles and release sites), no 50 

coherent model framework has been developed to explain or predict spontaneous release 51 

frequency. Developing a model framework has been difficult because changes in spontaneous 52 

release mechanics have been shown to be unrelated to changes in stimulated release. 22 We 53 

recently showed that an entropic force model framework can predict spontaneous release 54 

frequency. 28 This framework is based on the theory that the energy density of the NT carrying 55 

synaptic vesicles (SVs) exerts force on the SVs tethered at the membrane, driving spontaneous 56 

fusion events similar to an ideal gas exerting pressure on the walls of a container. Further, using 57 

this framework, we showed that amyloid-beta mutations alter spontaneous release frequency 58 

during homeostasis. 59 

Interestingly, spontaneous NT release has also been shown to mediate synaptic 60 

plasticity. 22,28–30 For example, long-term potentiation (LTP) protocols have been shown to induce 61 

a short-term, significant increase in spontaneous release frequency, followed by a slow 62 

reduction back toward baseline; however, the post-LTP induction release frequency remains 63 

above baseline. 31 Separately, SV distribution and density have also been shown to change >30 64 

min after LTP induction. 32,33 While these recent studies have used high-resolution electron 65 

microscopy, few have examined the early time-course of dynamic changes, which may not 66 

follow a simple trend and can have long-reaching implications. This gap in understanding the 67 

time-course of plasticity is essential to overcome to fully understand how plasticity occurs. 68 

Further, a single coherent framework that can predict how presynaptic spontaneous release 69 

mediates plasticity, or vice versa, has yet to be distinguished. Establishing a time-dependent 70 

predictive framework is essential because deficits in both spontaneous release and plasticity 71 

have been shown to correlate with neurodegeneration. 34–36 

> 72

In this study, we distinguish how SV spontaneous release frequency changes during 73 

early stages of presynaptic plasticity using our entropic force model. We use cultured primary 74 

hippocampal mouse neurons as a model system. We first induce synaptic plasticity using an 75 

established theta-burst protocol shown to correlate with associative memory, 37 changes in SV 76 

distribution, 32,33 and spontaneous release. 31 We use electron microscopy to show that SV 77 

distribution and density change dynamically over time and with synapse size. Based on SEM 78 

results, we predict changes in spontaneous release frequency using the entropic force 79 

framework and. We then use an experimental pHluorin-VGlut1 approach to support entropic 80 

force theory predictions that show spontaneous release frequency dynamically changes, 81 

consistent with a change in SV density. We use a single SV SGC5 labeling approach to show 82 

that SV mobility is constrained within the presynapse, and the dynamically changing SV number 83

To determine how spontaneous release rates change dynamically during plasticity, we 225

release intensity (q) and release probability (pr) based on the experimentally measured values 228 

during spontaneous release and 1 Hz stimulated release data, respectively. We then input those 229 

parameters in the multinomial model to fit the 40 Hz pHluorin-VGlut1 intensity while allowing the 230 

number of release sites and SVs in the recycling pool to vary as fit parameters. We then 231 

compared experimentally measured parameters (spontaneous release rate, stimulated release 232 

rate, and total SV number). 233 

We next established if the number of release sites dynamically changed during plasticity 234 

and observed that there is a slight, but not statistically significant, decrease in the number of 235 

release sites at 10 min (Blue Circles, Fig. 3H ) and 20 min after induction (Orange Stars, Fig. 236 

3H ), relative to control (Black squares, Fig. 3H ). We hypothesize that this is because the 237 

number of SVs increases faster than the active zone release site formation. This may meaning 238 

that, for a given presynapse, the number of SVs increases during plasticity while the number of 239 

release sites remains constant, resulting in a lower measured release site number during 240 

pHluorin-VGlut1 ( Fig. 3H ). 241 

We next measured the spontaneous release frequency as a function of release site 242 

number to determine if spontaneous release mechanics changed independent of release site 243 

mechanics. We observed a significant increase in spontaneous release frequency for both 10 244 

min (Blue Circles, Fig. 3I ) and 20 min after induction (Orange Stars, Fig. 3I ), relative to control 245 

(Black squares, Fig. 3I ). However, the spontaneous release frequency remained constant 246 

above 10 release sites for both 10 min and 20 min data (Solid Lines, Fig. 3I ). Based on entropic 247

decreases at synapses with larger active zone sizes (Solid Lines, Fig. 3I ). 249

# Discussion: 409 

In this study, we show that the presynaptic spontaneous release rate is dynamically 410 

modulated during synaptic plasticity via changes in SV pool density, and the dynamic changes 411 

follow the entropic force model. We connected the dynamically changing presynaptic SV 412 

number with the inter-synaptic vesicle exchange (ISVE) pathway to show how presynapses can 413 

up/down regulate their SV pools (Supplementary Material, Fig. S3 ). Importantly, we showed 414 

how the dynamically changing spontaneous release rate is altered in the presence of the P301L 415 

tau mutation and may represent an early-stage pathway to neurodegeneration. These results 416 

both support previous observations of changing SV distribution 33 and total SV number 62 during 417 

plasticity, as well as provide significant new insight into the mechanics and consequences of 418 

these changes. 419 

Mechanism of Dynamically Changing SV Number: 420 

It has been well established that the actin cytoskeleton is the dominant structural 421 

mechanism that maintains SV number and turnover within the presynapse. Actin is essential for 422 

maintaining and regulating SV release during and after activity. 47,63,64 Actin is important for SV 423 

recycling mechanics to maintain the recycling pool. 65,66 We, along with many others, have 424 

shown that actin also maintains SV exchange between the presynapse and the surrounding 425 

axon. 46,67–74 Thus, we hypothesize that actin dynamics during plasticity are the main mechanistic 426 

process by which SV number within presynapses changes dynamically. This hypothesis is 427 

consistent with, and ties together into a single coherent pathway, all previous observations on 428 

the role of actin before, during, and after activity. Further, this hypothesis also explains previous 429 

observations of up- or down-regulated SV pool sizes during LTP/LTD. There is a growing body 430 

of evidence showing changes in SV number and distribution during plasticity, and future studies 431 

should explore the specific role of actin structure/dynamics in regulating these dynamic 432 

changes. 433 

Meaning of Dynamically Changing SV Number: 434 

The role of dynamically changing SV numbers and pool distributions provides new 435 

insights into a combination of studies on synaptic plasticity. For example, previous studies have 436 

shown that the spontaneous release rate dynamically changes, 31 total SV number changes, 32,33 

> 437

SV protein turnover rate changes, 75 the overall size of pre/post synapse structure, 76 and more 438 

recently, that the actin network dynamically restructures due to activity. 64 However, these 439 

studies, which utilize post-synaptic measures (such as electrophysiology or PSD-95), 19,21,30 or 440 

measure limited aspects of presynaptic changes using advanced fluorescent microscopy 441 

techniques. 27,77 Our combined approaches in this study provide a mechanistic pathway by which 442 

all of these changes coordinate together to mediate presynaptic changes during plasticity: (i) the 443 

actin network restructures to more tightly bind SVs in the reserve pool; (ii) the increase in actin 444 

leads to more ISVE SVs to leave the axon and enter the presynapse increasing the density of 445 

SVs in the recycling pool; (iii) the increase in SV density leads to an increase in spontaneous 446 

release rates; (iv) the increase in spontaneous release rate leads to more SV recycling and thus 447 

protein degradation; (v) the actin network slowly depolymerizes releasing SVs from both the 448 

reserve pool and the recycling pool (used in step (iv) and replacing them with newer SVs). This 449 

single, coherent mechanistic pathway provides a framework for understanding the complex set 450 

of presynaptic changes that occur during plasticity. 451

Value and Significance of Entropic Force Framework: 453 

We have previously shown that the entropic force framework can explain the complex 454 

biological processes during homeostasis that lead to the observed spontaneous release rate. 455 

Here, we show that the same theoretical framework can both explain and predict changes 456 

during plasticity. This approach significantly advances our understanding of plasticity because 457 

the location of presynaptic release sites has been shown to align with postsynaptic receptor 458 

sites. 2,22 Further, spontaneous release has been shown to correlate with the strength of plastic 459 

changes that occur. 18,19,21 The results in this study show that presynaptic changes in 460 

spontaneous release rate during plasticity can be modeled using entropic force theory. This 461 

suggests that the strength of postsynaptic changes, and thus changes in synaptic weight, can in 462 

turn be modeled more effectively. Future studies should explore how the temporal changes 463 

observed in this study relate to spatial changes in spontaneous release locations and, in turn, 464 

changes in postsynaptic receptor response. 465 

Consequences of P301L During Plasticity 466 

The dynamic changes observed in normal neurons are inhibited during plasticity in the 467 

presence of P301L tau. This is consistent with previously observed changes in synaptic 468 

plasticity using ePhys approaches, 48,50,59 as well as reductions in synaptic transmission in later 469 

stages of neurodegeneration. 48 However, the results in this study provide a significant advance 470 

in our understanding of the mechanistic pathway by which reductions in synaptic function during 471 

plasticity occur during neurodegeneration. Our results suggest: (i) the mechanism of 472 

dynamically modulating SV number/density are inhibited in the presence of P301L tau; (ii) 473 

leading to a reduction in SV turnover during plasticity; (iii) reducing SV exocytosis/endocytosis 474 

efficiency; (iv) reducing synaptic function. The entropic force model provides a coherent 475 

theoretical framework for understanding and predicting how this process occurs. These results 476 

now provide specific molecular/mechanistic targets for future studies to explore to arrest or 477 

reverse the degenerative process. 478 

Limitations of the Present Study: 479 

While the present study provides a significant advancement in our understanding of 480 

presynaptic changes during plasticity, several limitations should be addressed in future studies. 481 

First, we used established hippocampal culture approaches that provide high spatial/temporal 482 

resolution but are limited because they do not replicate the exact circuit connections that occur 483 

physiologically. Second, the field stimulation approach we use in this study can induce a more 484 

dramatic change in SV dynamics and turnover than a more localized physiological response 485 

would induce. We note, however, that while our observed changes are likely amplified 486 

compared to physiological changes, we propose the same mechanisms still occur in 487 

physiologically relevant contexts, simply to a lesser degree. Third, the P301L tau results may be 488 

influenced by the known hyperexcitability process that occurs during the early stages of 489 

neurodegeneration. 50,58,59,78,79 Combined, these limitations can lead to a more dramatic 490 

response in cell cultures than would occur under physiological conditions. However, these 491 

limitations do not alter the underlying mechanisms or theoretical entropic force framework. 492 

Future studies should explore these same processes under physiological conditions and/or 493 

under different types of plasticity induction. 494

Computational Simulation Model of SV mobility: 716 

Presynapse structure: Simulated presynaptic vesicle pool mobility was performed using a 717 

lattice-based model approach in python. 28 Each site is designated as equal to the size of a 718 

single SV. Each time-step in the simulation is designated as 300 msec. The active zone is 719 

designated at one side of the lattice. The number of n-lattice sites along that side is equal to the 720 

number of release sites. We modeled the reserve pool as SVs fixed at lattice sites (> 5 sites for 721 

controls, and >3 lattice sites for 10 min, Fig. 1K ). Recycling pool SVs followed mobility rules that 722 

reproduced random diffusive motion with elastic collisions. If a SV in the recycling pool occupies 723 

an active zone site, then a spontaneous release event is registered using a Monte-Carlo 724 

threshold algorithm (p = 0.002 per hit). At the end of each simulation, the average time between 725 

recorded exocytosis events is recorded as a rate per min. After 100 simulations, the average 726 

spontaneous rate per minute is calculated along with SEM for the rate. 727

ISVE. Briefly, SVs that recently underwent exocytosis will leave the presynapse at a steady rate 1018
