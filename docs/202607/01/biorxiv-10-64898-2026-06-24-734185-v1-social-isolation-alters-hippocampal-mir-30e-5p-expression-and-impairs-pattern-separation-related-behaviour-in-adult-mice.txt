2 Abstract: 17 

Social isolation is commonly used to model social stress and is a known risk 18 factor for depression, with impacts on hippocampal function and postnatal 19 neurogenesis. However, most studies focus on social isolation in juvenile mice 20 isolation during adolescence, leaving the effects of prolonged adult isolation less 21 understood. Post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression by microRNAs 22 (miRNAs) plays a role in hippocampal function, and altered miRNA, as well as gene 23 expression, has been reported in the hippocampus of mice exposed to social 24

A single-nucleotide polymorphism in miR-30e in humans is associated with 25 increased expression of the mature miRNA, impaired cognition, 26 electroencephalogram waveform latency, depression, and schizophrenia. We 27 investigated whether adult isolation in mice alters gene regulation via microRNAs, 28 particularly miR-30e-5p, and affects hippocampal function. In adult BALB/c male 29 mice, 10 weeks of isolation increased miR-30e-5p expression in the ventral 30 hippocampus, reduced its target gene Neurod1 , and impaired hippocampal-31 dependent cognition (object pattern separation), without clear anxiety- or depression-32 like behaviours. Isolated mice also showed a blunted response to acute stress. 33 These findings suggest that adult social isolation affects hippocampal function 34 through post-transcriptional gene regulation, highlighting a role for miR-30e-5p in 35 neurogenesis and cognition in response to psychological stress. 36

Interconnected environmental and genetic risk factors associated with 45 cognitive, emotional, psychological and biological disturbances could constitutively 46 interact with each other to precipitate symptoms of MDD and/or anxiety (Grotzinger et 47 al., 2026). 3Social withdrawal is a common behaviour in anxiety and depression, and 51 social isolation a common risk factor for both (Kim et al., 2025; Wilkialis et al., 2021; 52 Zhu et al., 2024). Early work demonstrated that mice are sensitive to altered social 53 environment. Individual housing in mice induces behavioural changes compared 54 to standard laboratory conditions of group or paired housing (Valzelli, 1973). Thus, 55 the model has been widely adopted to assess the impact of a key psychological 56 stress in affective disorders, social isolation. Individually housed mice 57 display behaviours analogous to anhedonia and despair (Atrooz et al., 2021; Cryan 58 and Holmes, 2005; Scheggi et al., 2018). Affective-related behaviours and spatial 59 cognition also have previously been reported in adult mice that were exposed to 60 early life social isolation starting during adolescence (Hall et al., 1998; Hanif et al., 61 2025). Social isolation of rodents in early adulthood or old-age mimics aspects of 62 depression and anxiety (Aguiar et al., 2014; Magalhães et al., 2024; Mamedova et 63 al., 2024), impairs spatial memory (Zorzo et al., 2019) and stress response (Serra et 64 al., 2005). However, the neurobiological processes, psychological mechanisms and 65 molecular pathways affected by social isolation remain unclear. 66 The hippocampus plays a critical role in contextual and episodic memory 67 encoding/retrieval, which subsequently impacts negative bias of information 68 processing (Chen et al., 2019; Hamilton and Gotlib, 2008; Radzicki et al., 2025). 69 Should a negative or stressful experience occur in a context similar to a previously 70 encoded benign memory, the ability to recall events separately (despite contextual 71 similarity) is crucial to prevent catastrophic interference between benign and negative 72 memories and, in so doing, to avoid negative psychological biases (Egeland et al., 73 2015). Associative memory, which is the recall of memories from partial cues 74 and older memories during novel experiences, requires accurate encoding and recall 75 of prior memories in order to distinguish it from the newly encoded memories of the 76 present experience (Duncan et al., 2014). 5.6 Behavioural Assessment 198 

A battery of behavioural assessments was performed (Supplementary Methods). 199 Repeated measures of coat state (weeks 0-10) and sucrose preference (weeks 0, 2, 200 4, 6 and 10) were performed, with one-off assessments of open field behaviour 201 (week 6), olfaction (week 7), social dominance (week 8), object pattern separation 202 (week 8), splash test (week 9), novelty supressed feeding test (week 10) and 203 concluding with Porsolt swim test (week 10) (Supplementary Figure 1 and 2). 204 Procedures were carried out in half of the mice (n=10 individually housed and n=10 205 pair-housed) retaining the other half test-naïve for gene expression analysis. Cages 206 were not changed or cleaned within 24h of, or on the day of, testing. All behavioural 207 testing was performed during the light phase of the light: dark cycle between 09:00 208 and 17:00. During testing, all animals were placed in the start location within each 209 test, and after each trial, boli and urine were removed from the test arena, which was 210 subsequently cleaned with 1% Anistel® solution (Tristel Solutions Ltd, Suffolk, UK). 211 Light levels were task specific (described in each test in subsequent subsections), 212 but remained constant, within each test, for all experimental groups. All tests were 213 recorded using a camera positioned above the test arena, and for some tests the 214 animal’s movement was tracked using EthoVision software (Noldus Information 215 Technologies, Wageningen, The Netherlands). At the end of testing, mice were 216 returned to their home cage, and then to the housing room. An experimenter blind to 217 the housing group of the mice performed any live or post-acquisition scoring of the 218 behaviours. 219

5.10 Hippocampal tissue dissection and collection 253 

After 10 weeks of isolation, animals were euthenised by cervical dislocation and 254 decapitation. Brain samples were dissected and collected fresh into sterile, nuclease-255 free 1.5mL tubes (0030123328, Eppendorf, UK) and snap-frozen on dry ice. 256 Dissections were carried out to isolate frontal cortex, cerebellum and the 257 hippocampus. The hippocampus was further dissected into left ventral, left dorsal, 258 right ventral and right dorsal hippocampi. To do this, the whole hippocampi were 259 removed and dorsal/ventral and anterior/posterior orientation retained relative to the 260 rest of the brain with the hippocampus laid flat on the medial side. The ventral 261 hippocampus is identifiable by a small hump-like protrusion. To dissect the ventral 262 from dorsal, the hippocampus was divided into 5 equal sections from top (dorsal) to 263 bottom (ventral). The top two-fifths were dissected as one piece of tissue, while the 264 bottom 2/5s were dissected as another with the central remaining 5 discarded. The 265 top was taken as the dorsal hippocampus and the bottom as the ventral. Methods as 266 previously described by (Kelly et al., 2010) and dissections validated using 267 expression assay of gene Pde11a4 , which enriched in the ventral relative to dorsal 268 hippocampus. 269

In a test of pattern separation-like 648 cognition, socially isolated mice appeared to lose preference for exploration of an 649 object in a novel location despite initially showing similar exploratory behaviour to 650 pair housed mice. 651 The social isolation paradigm used, where mice are housed individually rather 652 than in pairs, is a unique approach since previous studies compared socially isolated 653 mice to groups of mice housed with as many as 15 cagemates (e.g. (Mudra 654 Rakshasa and Tong, 2020)). Group housed male mice are likely to be stressed given 655 the establishment of social hierarchies through aggressive behaviour seen between 656 male mice (Brown, 1953; Weber et al., 2022). Moreover, a majority 657 of previous reports investigating social isolation by individual housing have been done 658 earlier in development (post-weaning), the equivalent of early adolescence) (e.g. 659 (McGrath and Briand, 2022; Myers et al., 2024). In studies which investigate 660 adult rodents, social isolation in rats or transgenic animals, rather than inbred mice, 661 are more widely studied (Kim et al., 2025; Powell and Swerdlow, 2023; Toyoshima 662 and Yamada, 2023). In addition, tissue samples were carefully and separately 663 dissected from the dorsal and ventral hippocampus using a previously established 664 protocol (Kelly et al., 2010), given that the dorsal and ventral hippocampus are 665 functionally and neuroanatomically distinct substructures (Fanselow and Dong, 2010; 666 Henke, 1990; Moser and Moser, 1998).
